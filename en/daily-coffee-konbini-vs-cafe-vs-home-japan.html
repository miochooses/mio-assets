Your daily coffee in Japan: konbini (¥100) vs café (¥500) vs brewing at home

☕ For anyone in Japan who drinks a coffee a day and wonders whether the café habit is quietly draining the budget.
⏱ 30-second answer
  • Per cup: home ≈ ¥20–40, konbini ≈ ¥100–180, café ≈ ¥350–550. Over a year (one a day) that's roughly ¥10,000 vs ¥45,000 vs ¥150,000.
  • Konbini coffee is genuinely good and cheap. On pure cost, it already kills the case for a daily café run.
  • But the café isn't really selling coffee — it's selling a seat, wifi, and a workspace. If that's what you're buying, it's fair. If not, brew at home. See the full arrival checklist →

This is exactly the kind of small, everyday decision where "just grab one" quietly costs you money — because you make it 365 times a year. Mio doesn't care about vibes — here's the math.

The three options, per cup

A konbini coffee (7-Eleven, FamilyMart, Lawson) is typically ¥100–180 for a real drip cup, and honestly good. A café — Starbucks, Doutor, Tully's — runs about ¥350–550 for a small drip or latte. Brewing at home, drip or instant, works out to roughly ¥20–40 per cup once you average out the beans (illustrative — depends on what you buy).

The gap is the whole story. Switching a daily café habit to konbini saves roughly ¥100,000 a year. Switching it to home brewing saves about ¥140,000 a year — real money, from one small decision repeated.

What the café is actually selling

Here's the nuance Mio won't pretend away: at a café you're not paying ¥410 for coffee you could get for ¥120 at the konbini. You're paying for a chair, wifi, air conditioning, a table, and a place to sit for an hour. That's the real product. If you'd otherwise rent a co-working desk or just need somewhere to work, ¥410 for a seat is a fair price — the coffee is a rounding error.

So the honest question isn't "café vs konbini." It's: am I buying coffee, or am I buying a workspace? If it's coffee, the konbini already won. If it's the seat, pay for the seat and stop feeling guilty about it.

Home wins — but only if you'll actually brew

On paper, home is the runaway winner at ~¥10,000/year. The catch is behavioral: home only wins if you'll actually make it. A dusty drip machine and a bag of stale beans save nothing. If you know yourself and won't brew before leaving the house, the realistic contest is konbini vs café — and konbini wins that on cost every time.

Mio's verdict

Buying coffee → konbini. The ¥100 cup already killed the case for a daily café on pure cost. Will actually brew → home, and pocket ~¥140,000 a year. Buying a workspace → café is fine — just know that's what you're paying for. The "right" answer isn't about taste — it's about what you're really buying.
