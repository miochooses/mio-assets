"Kobe beef" is one of the most abused food labels in travel. Menus worldwide — and plenty in Japan — slap it on any marbled steak. But in Japan it's a protected, certified brand with a paper trail. Knowing that turns a ¥15,000 dinner from a gamble into a checkable purchase. Here's the money read.

What actually makes beef "Kobe"

Real Kobe beef must be certified Tajima-gyu (a Japanese Black lineage) born and raised in Hyogo Prefecture, meeting strict standards for marbling, meat quality grade and weight. Certified beef gets a Nojigiku seal and a 10-digit identification number you can trace back to the individual animal. Restaurants and butchers that are officially registered can serve it as Kobe beef; others can't legally use the certification. That seal is the whole point — it's what separates ¥15,000 real from ¥3,000 "Kobe."

How to make sure it's real

Two reliable moves: book a restaurant that appears on the official certified-Kobe list, or ask to see the certificate / seal / 10-digit ID — genuine places display or provide it happily. Lunch at a certified restaurant is the budget-smart way to try the real thing without the ¥25,000 dinner. If a place dodges the question or the price seems too good, you're probably paying Kobe money for non-Kobe beef.

Mio's verdict

Decide which you actually want. If it's real Kobe beef, treat it as a certified purchase: certified restaurant, seal, or ID — and go at lunch to cut the price. If you just want excellent Japanese wagyu, plenty of non-Kobe brands are outstanding and cheaper, so don't overpay for a "Kobe" label with nothing behind it. The only real mistake is paying ¥10,000+ assuming it's certified when nothing proves it is.
