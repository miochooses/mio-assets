Leaving Japan with money stuck on your Suica? The ¥500 deposit, the ¥220 fee, and the balance you never get back

🎫 For anyone flying home with leftover yen on a Suica/PASMO/ICOCA — before you leave your own money behind.
⏱ 30-second answer
  • Regular Suica (the ¥500-deposit card): return it and you get your ¥500 deposit back + the remaining balance minus a ¥220 handling fee — but only at JR East station windows (Tokyo/east Japan).
  • Welcome Suica (tourist card): no deposit and the balance is non-refundable — ever. It also expires 28 days after issue. Spend it to zero before you fly.
  • Because of the ¥220 fee and the JR-East-only rule, the simplest move is often to just spend the balance down at any konbini before departure — no fee, no counter queue.
  • Mobile Suica (in your phone): no physical card and no ¥500 deposit; you can keep it for a future trip or refund via the app.

At the end of a Japan trip, thousands of travelers fly home with ¥300, ¥800, sometimes ¥2,000 frozen on an IC card they'll never use again. There are ways to get it back — but they come with a deposit, a fee, and a location catch, and for small amounts the smartest move is different. Mio doesn't guess — here's exactly how each card works.

Regular Suica: deposit back, balance back — minus ¥220

A standard Suica is sold with a ¥500 deposit baked in. When you return the card at a JR East ticket window, you get:

Refund = ¥500 deposit + remaining balance − ¥220 handling feeIf your balance is under ¥220, the fee eats it and you simply get the ¥500 deposit back.

Two catches most tourists miss:

  • JR East only. A Suica is refunded by its issuer — JR East, i.e. Tokyo and eastern Japan. If you're flying out of Kansai (KIX) or elsewhere in the west, you generally can't refund a Suica there. Plan to do it before you leave JR East territory (or just spend it — see below).
  • The ¥220 fee makes small refunds pointless. With, say, ¥180 left, you'd only recover the ¥500 deposit — the balance is absorbed by the fee.

(PASMO and the western ICOCA work similarly with their own issuers/counters — refund the card with the company that issued it.)

Welcome Suica: nothing comes back

The Welcome Suica (the red tourist card with no deposit) is the trap. It has no ¥500 deposit to reclaim, and its remaining balance is completely non-refundable — before or after it expires. It also expires 28 days from issue. So any yen you don't spend is simply lost. If you're holding one, the only sensible plan is to run the balance down to ¥0 before you fly.

The move that beats the refund: spend it to zero

Because of the ¥220 fee and the JR-East-only rule, for most travelers the cleanest exit is to stop topping up near the end of your trip and spend the balance down. IC cards work at nearly every konbini (7-Eleven, FamilyMart, Lawson), vending machines, drugstores and many shops — so buy your last snacks, drinks and souvenirs on the card until it hits zero. No fee, no counter, no "wrong region" problem. Aim to land close to ¥0 on your last day.

Mobile Suica (Apple Wallet / Android): different rules

If your Suica lives in your phone, there's no physical card and no ¥500 deposit. It doesn't expire like the Welcome Suica, so you can simply leave it and reuse it on your next trip — or process a refund of the balance through the app. Check the current in-app refund terms (a handling fee may apply); for many repeat visitors, keeping it is the easy choice.

Mio's read

Don't donate your leftover yen to a plastic card. If you carry a regular Suica with real balance and you're still in JR East territory, return it for your ¥500 + balance − ¥220. If the balance is small, you're flying from the west, or you hold a Welcome Suica (non-refundable), just spend it to zero at a konbini instead. Have it in your phone? Keep it for next time. Rules and fees can change — confirm at a JR station before you rely on a refund.
