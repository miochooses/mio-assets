Is the Tokyo subway pass worth it? The break-even for tourists

🧳 For visitors deciding on a city transport pass — the Tokyo Subway Ticket vs just tapping an IC card.
⏱ 30-second answer
  • It's a break-even question, not a "get the tourist pass by default" one. The Tokyo Subway Ticket runs roughly ¥800 / ¥1,200 / ¥1,500 for 24 / 48 / 72 hours (illustrative — check current prices).
  • A single subway ride is about ¥180–330. So you need roughly 4–5 subway rides in a day to beat the 24-hour pass. Fewer than that, an IC card (Suica/PASMO) is cheaper and simpler.
  • The trap: the pass covers Tokyo Metro + Toei subways only — not JR (like the Yamanote Line). Ride a lot of JR and the pass loses value fast. See the Suica vs PASMO guide →

This is exactly the kind of small travel decision where "just grab the tourist pass" quietly costs you money — or saves it. Mio doesn't care about vibes — here's the math.

The two options, side by side

Option A is the Tokyo Subway Ticket, a tourist day pass giving unlimited rides on Tokyo Metro and Toei subway lines for a fixed window — roughly ¥800 (24h), ¥1,200 (48h), ¥1,500 (72h) (illustrative). Option B is a rechargeable IC card (Suica or PASMO): you tap in and out and pay per ride, about ¥180–330 depending on distance.

So a packed sightseeing day of 4–5+ subway hops around central Tokyo usually favours the pass. A relaxed day of 2–3 rides — or if you walk a lot between spots — and the IC card wins on cost and flexibility.

The trap most tourists miss: Tokyo has two subways + JR

Tokyo's rail map is run by several operators. There are two subway companies — Tokyo Metro and Toei — plus JR East, which runs the famous Yamanote Line loop and lines to places like Shibuya, Shinjuku, Tokyo Station and beyond. The Tokyo Subway Ticket covers only the two subways. It does not cover JR at all.

That matters because a lot of tourist routes lean on the Yamanote Line. If your day is half JR, you'd pay JR fares separately on top of the pass — quietly killing its value. An IC card covers everything seamlessly: Metro, Toei, JR, private railways, even buses and many shops, all with one tap. No thinking about which company's line you're on.

Osaka note: the "Amazing Pass"

Heading to Osaka too? The Osaka Amazing Pass is a different animal — it bundles unlimited subway/bus rides plus free entry to many attractions. That means its break-even depends on the sightseeing spots you'd visit anyway, not just rides. If you'll hit several paid attractions in a day, it can pay off well; if you're mostly wandering, do the attraction-entry math before buying (illustrative — check the current pass inclusions and price).

Mio's rule

4+ subway rides a day and staying central (mostly Metro/Toei) → get the day pass. A mixed JR + subway itinerary, or only light use → just tap an IC card. The "right" answer isn't the shiniest tourist ticket — it's whichever one your actual itinerary makes cheaper.
