Japan's tax-free shopping is changing: from Nov 1, 2026 you pay the tax first and claim a refund at the airport

🛍️ For travelers to Japan who are used to instant "tax-free" at the register — and want to know what's actually changing, and when.
⏱ 30-second answer
  • Japan's tax-free system is set to change on November 1, 2026. Instead of getting the 10% consumption tax knocked off at the till, you'll pay the full tax-included price and get the tax refunded after customs confirms your goods left the country. (This is the official plan per the Japan Tourism Agency; if you're reading this before that date, the old instant-exemption system still applies.)
  • Some rules get simpler: the split between "general goods" and "consumables" is abolished, the special sealed packaging for consumables goes away, and the ¥500,000 consumables cap is removed. The ¥5,000 (tax-excluded) per store, per day minimum stays.
  • The catch: you must keep the goods with you, show your passport at customs before checking bags, do it within 90 days of purchase, and not consume the items in Japan. See more Japan travel-money guides →

For years, "tax-free" in Japan meant the shop simply didn't charge you the 10% consumption tax — instant, at the counter. That's changing. Japan plans to move to a refund method, where you pay the tax and get it back only after leaving. Mio doesn't guess at rules like these; Mio reads the official Japan Tourism Agency notice and lays out what's confirmed, what's simpler, and what to watch. Here's the calm version.

What's the same right now (until the switch)

Until the new system takes effect, the current rules still apply. To shop tax-free you buy at a licensed tax-free shop, spend at least ¥5,000 (tax excluded) per store, per day, and show your passport (short-term visitor status). Purchases split into two buckets:

• General goods (clothes, bags, electronics, watches): usable in Japan, must be taken out within 6 months.
• Consumables (food, drinks, cosmetics, medicines): currently capped at ¥500,000, sold in special sealed packaging you're not supposed to open until you leave, and taken out within 30 days.

Old system: tax removed at the register (¥0 tax paid)Requires a licensed tax-free shop, ¥5,000+ tax-excluded per store per day, and your passport. Rules for goods sent overseas by international parcel changed in 2025 — confirm at the shop.

What changes from November 1, 2026 (planned)

According to the Japan Tourism Agency, from November 1, 2026 the system moves to a "refund method." The mechanics:

1) You pay the full tax-included price at purchase. No more discount at the till. Your passport is still presented and the purchase is logged electronically.

2) The tax is refunded after departure. Customs confirms your goods are actually being taken out of the country, then the consumption-tax amount is refunded to you — the refund is finalized once export is verified.

3) Categories get simpler. The distinction between general goods and consumables is abolished, the special sealed packaging requirement is removed, and the ¥500,000 consumables cap is removed. The ¥5,000 (tax-excluded) per store, per day minimum stays.

4) There's a time limit and a "don't use it" rule. You must export the goods (customs verification) within 90 days of purchase. And if you consume items in Japan (eating that food, using those cosmetics), they won't qualify for the refund at customs.

New system: pay tax now → refund after customs confirms exportPlanned effective date: November 1, 2026. Do the customs procedure BEFORE you check your bags — you can't do it after check-in.

Why Japan is doing this

The stated reason is anti-abuse: under instant exemption, some "tax-free" goods were resold inside Japan instead of being exported. Refunding only after customs confirms the goods leave closes that gap. For honest travelers it mostly means one extra step at the airport — and needing more cash/credit headroom up front, since you pay the tax and wait for it back.

How to plan for it

If you travel before Nov 1, 2026: nothing to do — the old instant-tax-free still works. If you travel on or after that date: budget for the full tax-included price at purchase, keep receipts and goods together, don't open/consume tax-free items you plan to claim, and leave extra time at the airport to do the customs step before checking your bags. Because details can still be finalized, check the official Japan Tourism Agency page close to your trip.
