Sumo tickets: that "¥38,000" seat is for FOUR people — here's what to actually book

🥋 For anyone who wants to see a grand sumo tournament and got scared off by the price on the booking page.
⏱ 30-second answer
  • The scary number is shared. Those ¥34,000–60,000 "masu" box seats are priced per box of four people — that's roughly ¥8,500–15,000 each, not per person on their own.
  • The cheap seat is real. A Western-style chair seat starts around ¥3,500 (weekday, upper level) and runs up to ~¥11,000 for the best chairs — a totally normal way to see a full day of sumo.
  • You can't wing it. Sumo happens just 6 times a year, 15 days each, in 4 cities, and popular Tokyo tournaments sell out — sometimes with no same-day tickets at all. Book ahead. More Tokyo ticket math →

A grand sumo tournament (honbasho) is one of the best-value spectacles in Japan — a full day of top-division sport for the price of a cinema-plus. But the booking page scares people off, because the headline price they see is the four-person box, not a single ticket. Here's the seat-by-seat money math, and what a solo traveller or couple should actually buy.

Seat types, decoded (and the per-person price)

SeatSticker pricePer person

Masu boxes are sold as a whole box for four — you pay the box price whether you fill it or not, so for one or two people they're poor value. Tamari (ringside) is a fixed ~¥20,000 per person and often restricted. For most visitors, a chair seat is the sane choice.

Why the "cheap ¥2,200 same-day" plan is a trap

Sumo does sometimes sell a small number of same-day general-admission tickets (~¥2,200) for the upper deck — but only if the tournament isn't sold out. Big Tokyo tournaments (Ryōgoku Kokugikan, in January, May and September) routinely sell out all 15 days, and in that case there are no same-day sales at all. Turning up hoping for a cheap ticket can mean not getting in. If your dates are fixed, book a reserved chair seat in advance.

The seat to book for most tourists: chair seat ~¥3,500–5,000pp, not the ¥38,000 box (that's ÷4)A full day of top-division sumo for the price of a nice lunch out — as long as you reserve ahead instead of gambling on same-day.

When it's on — plan around the 6 tournaments

Grand sumo runs six 15-day tournaments a year, rotating cities: Tokyo (January, May, September, at Ryōgoku Kokugikan), Osaka (March), Nagoya (July) and Fukuoka (November). If your trip doesn't overlap a tournament, the alternative is a morning-practice (keiko) viewing or a sumo experience — a separate, smaller-ticket activity that runs year-round in Tokyo.

Mio's verdict

Don't let the box price scare you. The ¥34,000–60,000 you saw is a four-person box (~¥8,500–15,000 each). For one or two people, book a reserved chair seat from ~¥3,500 on a weekday and you get the same full day of sumo for a fraction of the "sticker." Reserve ahead — especially for Tokyo — and don't rely on same-day tickets. No tournament during your trip? A morning-practice viewing is the year-round fallback.
