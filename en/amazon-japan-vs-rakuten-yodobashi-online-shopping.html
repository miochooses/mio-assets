Amazon Japan vs Rakuten vs Yodobashi — the cheapest way to buy online as a resident

🛒 For residents in Japan deciding where to shop online — and whether "points" actually make Rakuten cheaper.
⏱ 30-second answer
  • Amazon = fast, simple, English UI, Prime. Usually the lowest sticker price and the least friction.
  • Rakuten = higher list prices, but heavy points (SPU + campaign days). The after-points price can beat Amazon — but only if you're already in the Rakuten economy (their card/mobile) and will actually spend the points.
  • Yodobashi = ~10% points + free shipping, strong for electronics. Compare the effective price, not the sticker. Which card fits this →

This is exactly the kind of decision where "Rakuten gives more points, so it's cheaper" quietly costs you money — because points aren't cash, and higher list prices hide the trade. Mio doesn't care which app you like. Here's the math.

The one rule: compare the effective price

Sticker price is not what you pay. The number that matters is the effective price — sticker minus the points you'll actually use:

Effective price = sticker price − (points earned × the chance you'll really spend them)Illustrative. If you never spend the points, their value is ¥0 and the sticker price is the real price.

Points only count if you'll spend them before they expire. A ¥10,000 item that "gives back ¥1,500 in points" is only ¥8,500 if those points get used on something you'd have bought anyway. Otherwise it's still ¥10,000.

The trap most people miss: the point trap

Two ways points quietly cost you money:

1. Expiry. Many points (especially campaign/limited points) expire — sometimes in weeks. Points you never spend are worth exactly ¥0, which means you paid the full sticker price after all.

2. Buying more to earn more. The classic "point trap": you add a ¥3,000 item you didn't need to hit a campaign threshold and earn ¥900 in points. You didn't save ¥900 — you spent an extra ¥2,100. Points can only reduce the cost of things you were going to buy anyway.

Mio's verdict

Not in the Rakuten economy (no Rakuten card/mobile) → Amazon for simplicity and the lowest honest price. Buying electronics → Yodobashi for steady 10% + free shipping. Deep in the Rakuten ecosystem and it's a campaign day → Rakuten can genuinely win — but only on items you already planned to buy, and only if you'll spend the points before they expire. Compare the effective price, never the sticker.
