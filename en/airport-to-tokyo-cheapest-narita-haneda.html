Narita & Haneda to central Tokyo — the cheapest vs fastest way in (2026)

🧳 For anyone who just landed at Narita or Haneda and is deciding how to get into the city — right now, bags in hand.
⏱ 30-second answer
  • From Haneda: almost always take the train. Keikyu or the Tokyo Monorail is ~¥500–650 and fast — Haneda is close in, so paying for a bus or taxi rarely makes sense.
  • From Narita: pick by your goal. Skyliner (~¥2,500) is fastest to the northeast (Ueno); N'EX (~¥3,000) serves the west and has a round-trip tourist deal; Access Express or bus (~¥1,300) saves money if you're not in a hurry.
  • Get an IC card (Suica / Pasmo) first — it works on almost everything after you arrive. Suica vs Pasmo for tourists →

This is the first money decision of your trip, made while jet-lagged in an arrivals hall — exactly when people overpay. Mio doesn't care that the taxi looks convenient. Here's the cost-vs-time math for both airports.

Haneda → central Tokyo

Haneda (HND) is the easy one: it's much closer to central Tokyo than Narita, so the cheap option is also nearly the fastest. Two trains leave straight from the terminals — Keikyu Line (toward Shinagawa) and the Tokyo Monorail (toward Hamamatsuchō) — both roughly ¥500–650 and around 15–30 minutes to a major hub, where you transfer to your line.

Mio's rule for Haneda: take the train. The bus or taxi only wins if you have heavy luggage and want zero transfers, or it's late and trains have stopped.

Narita → central Tokyo

Narita (NRT) is ~60 km out, so here the money question is real: speed costs more. Four sensible options, from premium-fast to budget-slow. Note the two express trains split by direction — Skyliner runs to the northeast (Nippori/Ueno), N'EX to the west/south (Tokyo, Shinjuku, Yokohama).

Mio's rule for Narita: going northeast and want speed → Skyliner. Heading west/south, or two of you doing a round trip → N'EX (the round-trip tourist deal narrows the gap). On a budget and not rushing → Access Express or the bus at roughly half the price.

The thing to do before you queue: get an IC card

A Suica or Pasmo IC card works on Keikyu, the Monorail, Keisei Access Express, the metro, buses, and convenience stores — tap and go, no fumbling with ticket machines each transfer. The reserved express trains (Skyliner, N'EX) still need a separate seat ticket, but IC covers essentially everything else. Grab one at the airport station before you head in. Mio's verdict

Haneda → train, ~¥500, done. Narita → Skyliner if speed matters northeast, N'EX for the west or a round trip, Access Express/bus to save ~¥1,200 if you have the time. The "best" way in isn't the fastest or the cheapest — it's the one that matches where you're going and whether your clock is worth ¥40 a minute today.
