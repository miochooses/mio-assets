Tax-free shopping in Japan for tourists — how the 10% refund actually works (and the 2026 change)

🧳 For short-stay foreign visitors shopping in Japan and wanting the consumption-tax refund — without wasting time on purchases too small to bother with.
⏱ 30-second answer
  • Tax-free = you skip Japan's 10% consumption tax on eligible shopping. You need a passport, must be a short-stay visitor (not a resident), and must spend ¥5,000 or more (before tax) at one shop, in one day. (Official; verify current figures below.)
  • Big change ahead: from 1 November 2026 (planned), Japan switches to refund-at-departure — you pay the full tax-included price in the shop and claim the refund at the airport on your way out. Verify the current system before you rely on either.
  • Mio's rule: it's worth it on bigger buys; for a ¥5,000 purchase you save ~¥450–500 — decide if that beats the queue. See the arrival checklist →

Tax-free shopping sounds like free money, but it's a rules question, not a vibe. Mio doesn't guess — here's exactly who qualifies, what the minimum is, and what changes at the end of 2026. All figures are from official Japanese sources; rules like these change, so treat this as a map, not a guarantee.

Who qualifies: visitor, not resident

Tax exemption is for temporary foreign visitors — broadly, non-residents on a tourist visa or visa exemption who have been in Japan under six months. If you live or work in Japan (a resident), you generally don't qualify, even with a foreign passport. You show your passport at the time of purchase, and the tax-free goods must leave Japan with you. (Verify your own eligibility against the official pages below — status rules have edge cases.)

The current system: 10% off at the register

Today, at a licensed "Tax-Free" shop, the tax is handled at the point of sale — you either pay the tax-excluded price at the cashier, or get it refunded at the shop's tax-free counter right after. The consumption tax is generally 10% (8% on food and non-alcoholic drinks). The minimum is ¥5,000 or more (before tax) at the same shop on the same day.

General goods vs consumables (sealed)

Under the current rules there are two buckets, and — importantly — you can't combine them to reach the ¥5,000 minimum:

CategoryExamplesCurrent rule
General goodsClothes, bags, electronics, watches¥5,000+ before tax; use them freely; take them out of Japan
ConsumablesCosmetics, food, drinks, medicines¥5,000+ before tax; sealed in special packaging you must not open in Japan; take them out

The two thresholds are counted separately — ¥3,000 of cosmetics plus ¥3,000 of clothing does not make a qualifying ¥5,000 in either bucket.

The coming change: refund-at-departure (planned 1 Nov 2026)

Japan has announced a move to a "refund method." Under it (planned start 1 November 2026 — please verify the current status before your trip), you would:

Current systemPlanned new system (from ~Nov 1, 2026)
When you pay taxTax removed at the registerYou pay the full tax-included price in the shop
Getting the money backImmediate, in-storeRefunded at the airport/seaport when Customs confirms goods are leaving Japan

The departure customs check

Whichever system is in force, the refund is tied to actually exporting the goods. Under the new departure model, you present your passport at a tax-free terminal in the international departure area, and Customs may inspect — so keep your tax-free purchases in your carry-on (not checked luggage) until you've completed the procedure. Do the refund step before you check your bags.

When tax-free actually saves you money

Saving ≈ price (before tax) × 10%Illustrative. Food/soft drinks are 8%. The ¥5,000 minimum is per shop, per day.

On a ¥5,000 purchase you save roughly ¥450–500. On a ¥50,000 camera, about ¥4,500 — clearly worth queuing at the tax-free counter. On tiny buys that scrape the ¥5,000 line, weigh the saving against the time, the paperwork, and (under the new system) an extra airport step. Mio's verdict: batch your bigger shopping at one tax-free store to clear ¥5,000 easily, and don't chase the refund on trivial amounts.
