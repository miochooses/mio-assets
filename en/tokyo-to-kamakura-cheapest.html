Tokyo to Kamakura: the ¥940 JR train — and when a pass beats it

🌊 For anyone doing Kamakura (Great Buddha, Enoden, Enoshima) from Tokyo and picking a ticket.
⏱ 30-second answer
  • Just going to Kamakura from Tokyo? The direct JR Yokosuka Line is the cheapest — about ¥940 one way, ~1 hour, no transfer.
  • Hopping the scenic Enoden (Kamakura ⇄ Enoshima) a lot? The Enoden "Noriorikun" 1-day pass is ¥800 — worth it at roughly 3+ rides.
  • Basing in Shinjuku and doing Kamakura + Enoshima? The Odakyu Enoshima-Kamakura Freepass (~¥1,640) bundles the round trip + unlimited Enoden/area. Have a JR Pass? Then JR is free.

Kamakura has two "cheapest" answers depending on your day. If you just want the Great Buddha and Komachi-dori, the JR Yokosuka Line straight from Tokyo Station is simplest and cheapest. If you'll ride the little Enoden along the coast to Enoshima several times, a day pass quietly wins.

Which ticket for your day?

Great Buddha + central Kamakura only: JR Yokosuka from Tokyo (~¥940), tap in with your IC card, done. Kamakura + the Enoden coast + Enoshima: get there on JR, then buy the ¥800 Enoden 1-day pass to hop Hase (Great Buddha), Enoshima and back freely. Staying near Shinjuku and doing the whole Enoshima–Kamakura loop: the Odakyu Freepass (~¥1,640) is the tidy all-in-one. JR Pass holders should just ride JR.

Mio's verdict

Kamakura-only from Tokyo: the ¥940 JR Yokosuka Line. Riding the Enoden 3+ times: add the ¥800 day pass. Shinjuku base + Enoshima: the ~¥1,640 Odakyu Freepass. Match the ticket to your route, not the other way round — and save the difference for shirasu-don.
