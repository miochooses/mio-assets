That ¥400 dish you didn't order in Japan isn't a scam — it's otoshi

🍶 For anyone who sat down at an izakaya, got a mystery small plate, and found a charge for it on the bill.
⏱ 30-second answer
  • It's not a rip-off. That small appetizer you didn't order is otoshi (お通し) — a compulsory cover charge, usually ¥300–500 per person, that confirms your seat. Locals pay it too.
  • You usually can't refuse it at a traditional izakaya — it's baked into the system. Some modern or tourist-facing places let you opt out if you ask before ordering.
  • Budget for it. A table of 4 is ¥1,200–2,000 in otoshi before you order a thing. Not a scam — just a line item to expect. (And no, you still don't tip.)

You sit down at an izakaya, order a beer, and a tiny dish you never asked for appears — then shows up on the bill at ¥300–500 a head. It feels like a tourist trap. It isn't. This is otoshi (お通し, also called tsukidashi), one of the most misunderstood charges in Japan. Here's exactly what it is, how much to expect, and where you can dodge it.

What otoshi actually is

Otoshi is a small appetizer served to every seated (usually drinking) customer, whether you ordered it or not — and it's added to the bill even if you don't touch it. Functionally it's a cover / seat charge dressed up as a dish: it pays for your seat, the space and the service at small bars and izakaya. It's legal, standard, and applies to Japanese customers exactly the same way. It is not aimed at foreigners.

The math: it adds up per head, not per table

Because otoshi is per person, a group feels it. Four people at ~¥400 each is ¥1,600 before a single order. It won't break your budget, but if you're izakaya-hopping across a night, three stops for two people is roughly ¥1,800–3,000 in otoshi alone. Know it's coming so it doesn't feel like a con at bill time.

Otoshi to expect: ~¥300–500 per person, per izakayaNot a scam — a legal cover charge that keeps small bars running. Budget ~¥500pp per izakaya and it's a non-event; get surprised by it and it sours the meal.

Where you can avoid it

Otoshi is an izakaya / drinking-spot custom. You generally won't get it at:

• Ramen shops, gyudon & teishoku chains, family restaurants, cafés and fast food — no otoshi.
• Many big tourist-facing izakaya chains skip it or let you decline.
• At a traditional izakaya, you can politely ask before ordering ("otoshi wa arimasu ka?") — but at most, it's simply part of sitting down.

Mio's verdict

Relax — it's not a scam. Otoshi is a ¥300–500-per-person cover charge that every izakaya customer pays, and it's the price of the seat, not a tourist tax. Budget about ¥500 per person per izakaya and it disappears into the noise. Want to skip it entirely? Eat where it doesn't apply — ramen, teishoku, family restaurants — or ask before you order at tourist-friendly spots. And watch upscale places for a separate ~10% service charge.
