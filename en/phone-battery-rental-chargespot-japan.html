Phone dying in Japan? Rent a battery for ~¥165 — don't buy one

🔋 For travelers in Japan whose phone hit 5% — the thing locals do that visitors don't know about.
⏱ 30-second answer
  • A dead phone in Japan is expensive in a different way: no maps, no IC-card tap, no translation, no QR pay. But you don't need to buy a power bank.
  • Rent one from ChargeSpot — battery-share stands are inside 7-Eleven, FamilyMart, stations, airports and malls. It's roughly ¥165 for <30 min, up to about ¥540 for a day (rates vary — check in-app), and you return it at ANY stand nationwide.
  • The one trap: return it within 120 hours (5 days) or you're charged around ¥4,080 and effectively keep it. Pay by credit card, PayPay, Alipay/WeChat — app or app-free.

This is the small thing every Japanese person just does, and most visitors have never heard of: when your phone is dying, you don't hunt for a café outlet or overpay for a power bank at the airport — you grab a rental battery from a shared stand, and drop it at a different stand later. In a country where your phone is your map, your train ticket and your wallet, that's a genuinely useful move.

When to rent vs bring your own

Rent if your phone just needs the occasional emergency top-up — it's cheap, and there's nothing to pack or carry home. Bring your own power bank if you're a heavy user (maps + video all day) who'd otherwise rent every afternoon; a ¥2,000–3,000 bank you already own pays for itself over a long trip. Either way, the mistake is letting the phone die and losing your map and your Suica.

Mio's verdict

Phone at 5%? Rent a ChargeSpot battery for ~¥165 at the nearest konbini and drop it at any stand later — don't panic-buy a ¥3,000 power bank. Just set a reminder to return it inside 5 days so the ~¥4,080 cap never hits you. This is the kind of quiet, local move that keeps a Japan trip running.
