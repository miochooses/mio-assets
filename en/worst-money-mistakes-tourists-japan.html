The 10 WORST money mistakes tourists make in Japan (with the math)

💸 The quiet ones that cost the most — ranked, with numbers, and what to do instead.
⏱ 30-second answer
  • The costliest Japan money mistakes aren't scams — they're defaults: paying in your home currency, buying the ¥50,000 JR Pass on autopilot, and treating far-flung sights as easy day trips.
  • Fixing just the top 3 can save a couple tens of thousands of yen on a normal trip — no downgrade to your experience.
  • Each one below links to the full math. Skim the list, fix the ones that apply to you.

Japan isn't a rip-off — but a handful of default choices quietly drain tourists' budgets. None of these is a scam; they're just what happens when you don't do the math. Here are the ten that cost the most, ranked, with what to do instead.

MISTAKE 1 · costs 5–8% of every card purchase
Paying in your home currency (DCC)

When a terminal or ATM offers to charge in your currency instead of yen, it quietly adds a 5–8% markup. Always choose YEN. Cash vs card → · the ATM/DCC trap →

MISTAKE 2 · up to ~¥20,000 wasted
Buying the ¥50,000 JR Pass on autopilot

The 7-day pass jumped ~70% in 2023 to ¥50,000. It only breaks even at ~2 long round trips. One city or a relaxed trip? Pay per ride. Is the JR Pass worth it? →

MISTAKE 3 · ~¥9,920 to save an hour
Assuming the JR Pass covers the fastest train

The pass does not cover Nozomi/Mizuho without a ~¥4,960 each-way supplement. The free Hikari is only ~30 min slower Tokyo–Osaka. The Nozomi trap →

MISTAKE 4 · up to 3× the room rate
Booking hotels in the wrong week

Cherry blossom, Golden Week, Obon, New Year and autumn leaves spike hotels up to 3×. Shift your dates a week and save big. Peak-week timing →

MISTAKE 5 · a ~¥20,000+, ~half-day surprise
Day-tripping the snow monkeys from Tokyo

Entry is ¥800, but from Tokyo it's a ~12-hour, ~¥22,800 round trip. From Nagano, the ¥5,100 Snow Monkey Pass wins. Snow monkey math →

MISTAKE 6 · ~¥21,000 for one sight
Day-tripping Miyajima from Osaka/Kyoto

The floating torii is nearly free on the island, but the Kansai day trip is ~¥21,000 and ~10 hours. Base in Hiroshima instead. Miyajima math →

MISTAKE 7 · panic-skipping a great, cheap day
Thinking sumo costs ¥38,000

That scary price is a masu box for four (~¥8,500–15,000pp). A chair seat starts around ¥3,500. But Tokyo sells out — book ahead. Sumo ticket math →

MISTAKE 8 · buying a pass that no longer pays
Chasing Kyoto's dead ¥700 bus pass

The famous ¥700 bus day pass was abolished. It's ¥230/ride now, or a ¥1,100 subway+bus pass that only wins at ~5 rides. For 2–4 rides, just tap an IC card. Kyoto transport →

MISTAKE 9 · a wide, avoidable exchange markup
Changing money at the airport counter

Airport exchange counters bake in a wide markup. Withdraw yen from a 7-Eleven / Japan Post ATM with a fee-light card instead. Cheapest yen →

MISTAKE 10 · a shock at the register
Not budgeting the otoshi / new tax-free rules

Izakaya add a ¥300–500pp otoshi cover charge (not a scam). And from Nov 1, 2026 in-store tax-free ends — you pay the 10% upfront and refund at the airport. Otoshi explained → · Tax-free 2026 →

Mio's verdict

You don't have to travel cheap to stop losing money — you just have to not travel on autopilot. Fix mistakes 1–3 (pay in yen, do the JR Pass math, skip the Nozomi supplement) and you've likely saved ¥20,000–30,000 without changing a single thing you actually do.
