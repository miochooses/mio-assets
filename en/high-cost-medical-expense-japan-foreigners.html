High-cost medical expense benefit in Japan (kōgaku ryōyōhi) — the monthly cap foreigners keep missing

🏥 For any foreign resident enrolled in a Japanese health insurance (National Health Insurance / kokuho, or employees' shakai hoken) who's worried about a big hospital bill.
⏱ 30-second answer
  • Japan's High-Cost Medical Expense Benefit (kōgaku ryōyōhi, 高額療養費) caps your out-of-pocket medical cost per calendar month by income bracket. Anything above the cap is covered.
  • Get a limit certificate (gendogaku tekiyō nintei-shō) — or use a My Number card linked as your health card — and you pay only the cap at the counter. No fronting a huge sum and waiting for a refund.
  • Hit the cap 3+ times in 12 months and your cap drops further (multiple-month reduction, tasūkai gaitō). This is why some private medical insurance may be redundant →

This is exactly the kind of rule where not knowing costs you real money — either in panic-buying private insurance you don't need, or in fronting a scary hospital bill you never had to. Mio reads the official Japanese source and gives you the insider read.

How the monthly cap works (by income)

If you're enrolled in any Japanese public health insurance, you already pay only 30% of most medical costs. Kōgaku ryōyōhi goes further: it puts a ceiling on that 30% within a single calendar month, calculated per person per medical institution and by your income bracket. Cross the ceiling and the excess is refunded (or, with a certificate, never charged). Lower income → lower cap; higher income → higher cap, but still capped.

The trick: don't front the cash (limit certificate / My Number card)

Left alone, you'd pay the full 30% at the counter and claim the excess back later — a refund that can take weeks. Avoid that entirely:

Option A — limit certificate. Ask your insurer in advance for an Eligibility Certificate for Ceiling-Amount Application (gendogaku tekiyō nintei-shō, 限度額適用認定証). Show it at reception and the counter charge is capped on the spot.

Option B — My Number card. If your My Number card is registered as your health insurance card (maina hoken-shō), a participating hospital can read your cap directly — so you pay only the cap with no separate certificate and no yearly renewal. This is the low-effort path for most residents.

Multiple-month reduction (tasūkai gaitō)

If your household qualifies for the high-cost benefit 3 or more times within the most recent 12 months, then from the 4th time your monthly cap drops to an even lower amount. So a long or repeated illness gets cheaper per month the longer it runs — the opposite of what people fear.

Why this can make private medical insurance redundant

Many foreign residents buy private "hospitalization" insurance imagining a catastrophic, unlimited bill. But with kōgaku ryōyōhi, a covered treatment that would cost ¥1,000,000 gross doesn't cost you ¥300,000 — it costs you roughly your monthly cap (e.g. ~¥80,000 for a standard earner), and less again after the 3rd month. Mio's verdict

If you're in any Japanese public health insurance, you're already protected by a monthly cap. Link your My Number card as your health card (or request the limit certificate before a planned admission), and you'll never front a large sum. Then re-read any private medical insurance pitch with that cap in mind — a lot of it is covering a gap that doesn't exist.
