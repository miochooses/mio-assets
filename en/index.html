💴 Tax in Japan, calculated

Japan's resident tax (jūminzei) shock — the delayed bill that hits in year 2 and when you leave
The point: it's billed on last year's income and Jan-1 residency — so a surprise bill lands in year 2, and you still owe it when you quit or leave (you may need a tax representative).
Year-end adjustment (nenmatsu chōsei) vs filing a tax return — which you need, and the refunds foreigners miss
The point: your employer's December adjustment settles most single-job employees' tax — but side income, two jobs, mid-year quits, or medical/furusato/mortgage deductions mean filing to reclaim overpaid tax.
Furusato Nozei (hometown tax) for foreign residents — do you actually save?
The point: yes, you likely qualify (it's based on paying resident tax, not nationality). You pay ¥2,000 net and get gift-cap ~30% back — but only up to an income-based limit. Mio does the math.
Furusato nozei limit, calculated — how much can you donate before you LOSE money?
The point: it's only "net ¥2,000" up to a personal ceiling set by your income/resident tax — donate over it and the excess isn't deducted, a real loss. Use an official simulator.
Japan's income “walls” (¥1.23M / ¥1.06M / ¥1.30M), explained for dependents
The point: earning more can leave you with less — but only in specific yen zones. Mio shows exactly where the take-home dip is (and when crossing is still worth it).
The ¥1.06M / ¥1.30M social-insurance wall, calculated — the take-home dip and when crossing pays off
The point: cross it and a dependent spouse pays ~15% for their own pension+health, so gross rises but take-home dips until ~¥1.5–1.7M. A worked example (mid-reform — verify).
Remote work & foreign income while living in Japan — tax residency & double taxation
The point: Japan taxes you by residency category, not visa — remote work done from Japan is usually Japan-source, and remittances can pull in foreign income. Treaties + foreign tax credit stop double tax.

🏥 Health & pension

Shakai Hoken vs Kokumin Kenkō Hoken — which health insurance you're in, and what it costs
The point: employed → Shakai Hoken (employer splits the cost); not employed → National Health Insurance at city hall (income-based, you pay it all). Both cover ~70% — the real mistake is being in neither.
Changing jobs in Japan? The health-insurance gap that gets backdated
The point: you can't skip a day of coverage — enrol late and National Health Insurance backdates premiums to the day you lost the old plan. Bridge via NHI, nin'i keizoku, or a family dependent.
Do foreigners have to pay into Japan's pension? The "opt-out" myth, agreements & refunds
The point: you generally can't opt out — but student/low-income exemptions, totalization agreements, and the leaving-Japan lump-sum refund all protect your money.
Having a baby in Japan as a foreign resident — the real cost, the ¥500,000 lump-sum & subsidies
The point: normal delivery isn't insured, but the ~¥500,000 childbirth lump-sum, free check-up coupons, child allowance and income-based nursery pay most of it back — claim them in order.

📈 Investing & sending money home

Can foreign residents use NISA and iDeCo? Eligibility, the leaving-Japan catch & the US-citizen problem
The point: it's residency, not nationality — most foreign residents can open NISA/iDeCo. But iDeCo locks money until 60, leaving Japan breaks the tax perks, and US citizens are usually shut out.
US citizens investing while living in Japan — the NISA rejection, the PFIC trap, and what works
The point: most JP brokers reject US persons (FATCA), Japanese funds are PFIC US-tax landmines, and you still file FBAR/8938 — hold US-domiciled instead. Not advice; see a cross-border pro.
Sending money home from Japan the cheapest way — bank wire vs Wise vs remittance services
The point: the real cost isn't the fee — it's the hidden exchange-rate markup. Specialist services usually beat bank wires, but a Wise JPY account can't receive your salary.

💼 Work, visa & jobs

Can a dependent-visa spouse work in Japan? The 28-hour rule and the permission you need first
The point: a Dependent visa doesn't allow work by default — get the free "permission to engage in other activity" (28 hrs/week cap) first; full-time needs a work-visa change.
Lost your job on a work visa — how long can you stay, and will your visa renew?
The point: your visa stays valid until expiry, but the 14-day notification, a ~3-month non-activity revocation risk, and the "still doing the activity?" renewal check start ticking. Verify with Immigration.
Lost your job in Japan? Unemployment benefit (koyō hoken) for foreign residents
The point: you've paid koyō hoken every payslip — if you lose your job you may claim the basic allowance at Hello Work, but your residence status must still let you stay and job-hunt.
Converting your foreign driver's licence to a Japanese one (gaimen kirikae) — steps & who skips the test
The point: an IDP only lasts ~1 year for residents — convert via gaimen kirikae (licence + JAF translation + 3-month proof); whether you skip the written/practical test depends on your country.

Quitting or losing your job in Japan as a foreigner — the money & admin checklist
The point: leaving a job detaches your health insurance, pension and visa — switch each within days, grab your employer documents, and notify Immigration within 14 days.

Leaving Japan? Claim your pension lump-sum — and reclaim the 20.42% tax

🧺 Everyday money (small decisions, calculated)

Coin laundry vs buying a washing machine — which is cheaper for your stay?
The point: it's a break-even, not a preference. Stay under ~8 months → coin laundry; a year or more → buy (ideally secondhand and resell). Don't forget Japan's appliance disposal fee.
Konbini vs supermarket in Japan — what the daily habit really costs
The point: the convenience-store lunch feels tiny, but the same basket runs ~30–40% more than the supermarket. Mio turns the daily habit into a yearly number — and it's bigger than you think.
One good umbrella, or a ¥500 conbini vinyl every time it rains?
The point: it's a lose-rate question, not a preference. Buying vinyl 4–6×/year is ¥2,000–3,000 every year; a real umbrella pays for itself in ~1 year — unless you're a chronic umbrella-loser, in which case cheap is rational.
Furnishing your place: buy new, buy secondhand, or rent?
The point: for a 1–3 year stay, secondhand + resell before you leave usually wins (~¥21k net vs ~¥90k new). The number people forget is Japan's legal appliance disposal fee.
Suica/PASMO vs cash on the trains — and when a commuter pass (teiki) pays off
The point: tap an IC card over cash (a few yen cheaper, faster). But commute the same route ~15+ round trips a month and a teiki wins — buy the 6-month version.
Cooking at home vs eating out every day in Japan — what it really costs
The point: a gyūdon is ~¥600, cooking ~¥250 — one lunch out a day is ~¥90,000/year. Unless you're solo and time-poor, where waste + time can erase the gap.
100-yen shops (Daiso/Seria) vs Nitori vs recycle shops — kitting out an empty room
The point: 100-yen everything looks cheapest, but on daily-use items replacement frequency wins the bill. Mio splits the starter kit by cost-per-use.
Pocket WiFi vs home fibre vs tethering — which internet for your stay
The point: short stay / light use → pocket WiFi or SIM tethering (no install, no contract trap). Long stay + heavy use → home fibre is cheaper per GB, but watch the 2-year auto-renew and exit fees.
Konbini ATM vs your bank's ATM — how withdrawal fees quietly cost you
The point: ~¥110–330 per off-hours withdrawal feels trivial — which is why 2–4×/month quietly costs thousands a year. Fix: free-withdrawal counts, fewer larger withdrawals, or an online bank.
Vending machine vs supermarket vs your own tumbler — the daily drink habit
The point: one drink a day is ~¥58,000/yr from a vending machine, ~¥35,000 from a supermarket, ~¥11,000 from a tumbler — nobody notices ¥150, which is the trap.
Staying warm cheaply in a Japanese winter — aircon vs kerosene vs kotatsu
The point: Japanese apartments leak heat, so heating the whole room is the expensive option. Heat the body, not the air: a kotatsu plus layers beats blasting the aircon.
¥1,000 quick-cut vs a full salon — cost and the language barrier
The point: for a standard cut the quick-cut wins on both cost (~¥15k vs ~¥60k/yr) and language friction (no Japanese needed). Save the salon for colour or perm.
Amazon Japan vs Rakuten vs Yodobashi — cheapest online once points are counted
The point: compare the after-points effective price, not the sticker. Amazon wins on honest low prices; Yodobashi's ~10% suits electronics; Rakuten only wins if you're in its economy and actually spend the points.
Buying a bicycle vs share-cycle — the break-even, and the bōhan tōroku rule
The point: ride daily / stay 5+ months → buy a mamachari, but do the ¥500 anti-theft registration in your name or police flag it as stolen. Occasional / short stay → share-cycle.
Gym vs home workout vs the municipal gym — cost per actual visit
The point: a ¥9,000/mo gym used 4×/mo is ¥2,250 a visit — the ward gym at ~¥400 a drop-in (no contract) or free bodyweight at home wins unless you go 3×+ a week.
Tap water vs bottled water in Japan — is tap safe, and what's cheapest per litre
The point: Japanese tap water is safe per waterworks standards and costs ~¥0.2–0.5/L; bottled is 100–500× that. Drink tap, filter for taste, reserve bottles for convenience.
Cheapest way across Japan — Seishun 18 vs highway bus vs LCC vs Shinkansen
The point: there's no single cheapest mode — it's whichever wastes fewer hours than you can afford. Fast costs yen, cheap costs time; the 18 Kippu wins per-km, but only in season.
The ¥20,000 "switch and get cashback" (MNP) phone deal — do the numbers work?
The point: rarely. Compute plan × months + device − cashback over the lock-in, and a plain ¥1,000–2,000/mo MVNO usually beats the flashy cashback within a year.
Missed the last train — taxi, capsule hotel, manga café, or wait for first train?
The point: under ~3 km the taxi wins; past ~6 km a ¥2,000 net café + first train beats a ¥6,000 late-night fare. The real question is just how far you are.
Daily coffee — konbini (¥100) vs café (¥500) vs brewing at home
The point: konbini coffee already killed the daily café on cost (~¥45k vs ~¥150k/yr); home wins (~¥10k) if you'll actually brew. The café is really selling you a seat, not coffee.
Need a suit in Japan? Buy cheap vs rent — the break-even by number of uses
The point: wear it 2+ times → buy a cheap chain suit (¥15k–30k). A true one-off, especially a funeral → rent (¥5k–15k). Break-even ≈ 3 uses.
