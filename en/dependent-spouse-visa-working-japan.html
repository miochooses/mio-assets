Can a dependent-visa spouse work in Japan? The 28-hour rule and the permission you must get first

🛂 For spouses and family on a Dependent (家族滞在, Kazoku Taizai) visa who want to earn some income in Japan.
⏱ 30-second answer
  • A Dependent visa lets you live in Japan as a family member — but by default it does not allow paid work.
  • To work part-time you must first get "Permission to Engage in Activity Other Than That Permitted" (shikakugai katsudō kyoka). It's free, and it caps work at 28 hours per week — the same cap that applies to students.
  • Want a full-time job? That generally needs a change to a work visa, not the part-time permission. This is general info — always verify with the Immigration Services Agency. See the full arrival checklist →

This is one of those rules where guessing is expensive: work beyond what your status allows and you risk your visa. Mio doesn't guess — it reads the Immigration Services Agency's (ISA / moj.go.jp) own pages. Here's the plain-English read. It is general information, not legal or immigration advice — confirm your case with Immigration.

What the Dependent visa allows by default

The Dependent (Kazoku Taizai) status is for the spouse or children of someone in Japan on a work or student status, who are financially supported by that person. Per ISA, it covers "daily activities" as a family member — living together, going to school, everyday life. It does not include income-earning work on its own. So a spouse on a Dependent visa cannot legally take a paid job until they get the extra permission below.

The permission you must get first (shikakugai katsudō kyoka)

To do paid work, a Dependent-visa holder must first obtain "Permission to Engage in Activity Other Than That Permitted under the Status of Residence Previously Granted". Key points from ISA:

QuestionAnswer
CostFree (no application fee)
Work limit once grantedGenerally up to 28 hours/week
Where to applyRegional Immigration office, or at the airport on arrival (landing inspection)
Not allowedAdult-entertainment / "public morals" businesses (bars with hostessing, pachinko, etc.)
ProcessingOn the spot at the airport; otherwise roughly 2 weeks–2 months at an office

If you hold more than one part-time job, Immigration adds the hours together — the total still can't exceed the weekly cap.

What happens if you exceed the 28 hours (or skip the permission)

Working without the permission, or beyond the 28-hour cap, is working outside your status. That can count as unauthorized work and put your visa at risk — think trouble at your next extension or change-of-status, and in serious cases penalties or loss of status. The rule is not a suggestion; treat 28 hours as a hard ceiling and keep evidence of your hours. If anything about your situation is unusual, verify with the Immigration Services Agency before you start.

When you need to switch to a work visa instead

The part-time permission is exactly that — part-time. If your spouse wants a full-time job, the correct route is usually a change of status of residence to an appropriate work visa (for example, "Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services"), which depends on the job, your qualifications, and the employer. Once on a work visa, the 28-hour cap no longer applies, but you're then tied to that visa's permitted activities instead.

How this interacts with the income "walls"

Even within 28 hours a week, part-time income can bump into Japan's tax and social-insurance "walls" — the income thresholds where a dependent starts owing tax or has to enrol in their own health insurance and pension, and where the household's dependent deductions change. The visa cap and the money walls are separate rules: staying under 28 hours keeps you visa-compliant, but your yen earnings are what trigger the walls. Plan both together. Mio's verdict

Get the free permission before your first shift, keep total work at or under 28 hours/week, and avoid the prohibited industries. Want to work full-time? Plan a work-visa change instead of stretching the part-time rule. And watch the income walls in parallel — the visa and the tax thresholds are different meters.
