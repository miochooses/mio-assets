Fuji-Q's "Free Pass" costs ¥6,000+ — the break-even

🎢 For anyone heading to Fuji-Q who's about to auto-buy the pass without checking if they'll use it.
⏱ 30-second answer
  • Entry to Fuji-Q Highland is free. The "Free Pass" is the confusing bit — it's not free entry, it's an unlimited-ride pass at ~¥6,000–6,800 (adult, dynamic by date).
  • Individual coaster tickets are ~¥2,000 each for the big ones. So the pass only wins once you ride roughly 3+ major coasters — below that, pay per ride.
  • Mio's rule: big group of thrill-riders doing the whole park → Free Pass. Going mainly for 1–2 signature coasters (and the Fuji view) → pay per ride and save ¥2,000–4,000. Getting to the Fuji area →

Fuji-Q Highland is the big thrill park right under Mt Fuji, home to record-breaking coasters. The pricing trips people up because "Free Pass" sounds like free entry — it isn't. Entry is genuinely free; the Free Pass is the all-you-can-ride ticket. Whether it's worth ¥6,000+ comes down to one number: how many big rides you'll actually do.

How Fuji-Q pricing works

You walk in for free. Then you either buy a Free Pass (~¥6,000–6,800 adult, priced dynamically by date) for unlimited rides, or you pay per ride — the headline coasters run around ¥2,000 each, smaller attractions less. That's the whole decision: unlimited vs à la carte.

Free Pass ~¥6,000 = about 3 big coastersRide 3+ major coasters and the pass wins. Ride 1–2 and pay-per-ride is cheaper. Factor in that popular coasters have long queues, so most people realistically ride fewer big ones per day than they expect.

Mio's verdict

Count the big coasters you truly care about before buying. If it's the full thrill-park day with 3+ major rides, the Free Pass at ~¥6,000 is the right call. If you're really there for one or two signature coasters plus the Mt Fuji backdrop, skip the pass and pay per ride — you'll likely save ¥2,000–4,000. The classic mistake is auto-buying the Free Pass, queuing all day, riding two coasters, and paying triple what you used.
