Converting your foreign driver's licence to a Japanese one (gaimen kirikae)

🚗 For foreign residents in Japan who already drive back home and want to keep driving here legally — students, working-holiday, and long-term assignees.
⏱ 30-second answer
  • An International Driving Permit (IDP) is a short-term fix: it's valid for about one year from your entry into Japan and can't be renewed here. If you're staying longer, you convert your home licence — this is gaimen kirikae (外免切替).
  • You'll need your valid foreign licence, an official Japanese translation (e.g. from JAF), and proof you held the licence 3+ months in the issuing country before leaving.
  • Then it splits by issuing country: some countries are exempt from the exams (knowledge + eyesight check only); others must also pass a written test and a practical driving test. Which group you're in depends on your country — verify with your prefectural licence centre. See the full arrival checklist →

This is one of those admin jobs where a wrong assumption is expensive — an extra test-day trip, an unrenewable permit that quietly expires, or driving on a licence that's no longer valid. Why an IDP isn't enough for residents

An International Driving Permit lets short-term visitors drive, but for residents it's a trap that runs out. It's generally valid for about one year from your date of entry into Japan, and you can't renew an IDP inside Japan — you'd have to leave and re-enter, which authorities treat as gaming the rule. Once that year lapses, a foreign licence plus translation stops being valid to drive on. So if you live here, the durable answer is converting to a Japanese licence. (Rules and exact validity can change — verify with your prefectural licence centre.)

Documents you'll need

Requirements are set nationally but handled by each prefecture's police licence centre, so the exact list varies. Commonly you'll need:

DocumentNotes
Your foreign driver's licenceMust be valid — an expired licence can't be converted. An IDP alone is not accepted.
Official Japanese translation of the licenceTypically from JAF (Japan Automobile Federation) or an authorised consular body. JAF now takes translation requests online from within Japan.
Proof you held the licence 3+ monthsEvidence you stayed in the issuing country at least 3 months in total after getting the licence — usually shown via passport entry/exit stamps. This exists to stop "licence tourism."
Residence & ID documentsResidence record (juminhyo) / residence card / passport and a photo. Since Oct 2025 a residence record is required, so conversion is for registered residents.

The exact document set, fees, and photo specs depend on your prefecture and your country — confirm with your prefectural licence centre before you go.

The country split: test-exempt vs must-test

This is the part that decides how hard your day is. After the paperwork check, applicants fall into roughly two groups depending on the country (or, for some places, the state/region) that issued the licence:

Which group am I in? → It depends on your issuing country/region.Illustrative only. There is no universal list here — confirm your specific country's treatment with your prefectural licence centre or an official source.
GroupWhat's required
Test-exemptCertain countries/regions are exempt from the exams. You still do the document check and an eyesight/aptitude check, and answer basic knowledge questions, but you skip the full written and practical tests.
Must-testEveryone else must also pass a knowledge (written) test and a practical driving test at the licence centre, on top of the document and eyesight checks.

Whether your country is exempt varies, and the lists are set by the authorities and do change — so Mio won't hand you a country list to memorise. Depends on your country; verify with your prefectural licence centre or the National Police Agency guidance.

The practical test reality

If you're in the must-test group, don't underestimate the driving test. It's taken on the licence centre's own course and graded on Japanese-specific technique — exaggerated safety checks (mirrors and shoulder checks at set points), precise lane positioning, and hazard procedures — not just "can you drive." Experienced drivers commonly don't pass on the first attempt, and each retry is another booking, fee, and trip. Many people take a lesson or two on the actual test course first. Treat a first-try pass as a bonus, not the plan.

Mio's verdict

Don't lean on the IDP if you're staying. Start the gaimen kirikae early: get the JAF translation, gather your 3-month proof, and check whether your issuing country is test-exempt or must-test before booking. If you must test, assume a couple of attempts and consider a course lesson. The whole thing is much smoother when you know your country's path in advance — which, again, depends on your country: verify with your prefectural licence centre.
