Furikomi fees in Japan — the cheapest way to pay rent and send money by bank transfer

🏦 For foreign residents in Japan paying rent or sending money to another person by bank transfer (furikomi).
⏱ 30-second answer
  • A single bank transfer (furikomi) commonly costs ~¥110–770, depending on your bank, whether it's the same bank or another bank, and ATM vs online.
  • Online banks (Sony Bank, Rakuten Bank, Sumishin SBI Net Bank) often give you several FREE transfers per month; megabanks tend to charge more, and ATM transfers cost the most.
  • Mio's rule: for rent or recurring payments, use an online bank with free monthly transfers, or set up auto-debit (kōza furikae) — it's usually free. Opening an account as a foreigner →

Paying ¥770 to move your own money across town sounds absurd until you realize a megabank ATM can charge exactly that. Do it monthly for rent and it's a quiet subscription you never signed up for. Mio doesn't care how your bank "feels" — here's the math.

Why the same transfer has four different prices

The fee for one furikomi depends on three switches: which bank you're with, same bank vs other bank (transfers to the same bank are often cheaper or free), and ATM vs online/app (online is almost always cheaper than a teller or ATM). Stack the expensive options — a megabank, to another bank, at an ATM — and you hit the top of the range. Flip them and it can be free.

Worked example: paying rent for a year

Say rent is paid monthly by transfer. On a megabank, other-bank, at an ATM at ¥660 each, that's ¥660 × 12 ≈ ¥7,920/year, just in fees. Move the same payment to an online bank with free monthly transfers, or to auto-debit, and the fee drops to roughly ¥0. The rent didn't change — only how you moved it.

Over a typical 2-year lease that's roughly ¥15,000 saved for one setup change you make once.

The trap most people miss: paying rent the hard way

Two things quietly cost foreign residents money here. First, auto-debit (kōza furikae) — where the landlord or management company pulls rent automatically — is usually free and never charges you a per-transfer fee, unlike manual furikomi. If your contract allows it, it's almost always the cheapest option. Second, code-pay apps like PayPay are not a standard way to pay a landlord in Japan; rent is overwhelmingly bank transfer or auto-debit, so don't assume a QR app will work for it.

Smart setup: keep an online bank (free monthly transfers) for any manual sending, and put rent/utilities on auto-debit where offered. That combination gets most people to near-zero transfer fees.

Mio's verdict

Recurring payment (rent, utilities) → auto-debit, or an online bank's free monthly transfer. One-off transfer to a person → online bank, same-bank if possible, never a megabank ATM if you can avoid it. The "right" bank isn't the one with the nicest branch — it's the one that isn't metering every move of your own money.
