The Japan Expressway Pass is GONE — what road-trippers actually use now

🚗 For anyone renting a car in Japan and reading old blogs about a nationwide toll pass that no longer exists.
⏱ 30-second answer
  • The nationwide passes are discontinued. The famous Japan Expressway Pass (JEP) and the Central Nippon Pass (CEP) are no longer sold — ignore old guides that recommend them.
  • What remains: four REGIONAL unlimited-toll passes — Hokkaido, Tohoku, Kyushu, and San'in-Setouchi-Shikoku. You apply at the rental counter; tolls in that region become unlimited via a rental ETC card. (Note: the Tohoku pass stops accepting applications on 30 Sep 2026.)
  • For central Japan (Tokyo, Kansai, etc.) there is NO pass — you pay per toll (a rental ETC card is ~¥330–550) — and honestly, the trains usually beat driving there. JR Pass math →

Search "Japan toll pass" and you'll find years of blogs praising the all-you-can-drive Japan Expressway Pass. The problem: it's gone, and so is the Central Nippon version. If you plan a rental-car trip around a pass that no longer exists, you'll either overpay per toll or scrap the plan at the counter. Here's what actually exists in 2026.

Regional passes give unlimited expressway tolls in that region for a set number of days at a flat price, added on top of your car rental; they use an ETC card issued by the rental company (~¥330–550), so you glide through toll gates without stopping. Apply when you reserve the car.

Long regional drives rack up ¥10,000+ in tolls — a flat pass caps itDoing a multi-day Hokkaido or Kyushu road trip? The regional pass usually beats paying per toll. One-off short drives? Just take the ETC card and pay per gate.

When a car + pass wins — and when the train wins

Get the regional pass if you're doing a genuine road trip in Hokkaido, Kyushu, Tohoku or the San'in-Setouchi-Shikoku area — places where a car unlocks the countryside and tolls add up fast. Skip the car in the Tokyo–Kyoto–Osaka corridor: there's no toll pass, parking is pricey, and the Shinkansen/trains are faster and cheaper. Either way, always take the rental's ETC card — it's cheap, avoids cash at toll gates, and unlocks the passes and time-based toll discounts.

Mio's verdict

Don't plan around the Japan Expressway Pass — it's discontinued. For a Hokkaido/Kyushu/Tohoku/San'in road trip, get that region's Expressway Pass; for central Japan, take the train. Add the ETC card to any rental, and if you're eyeing Tohoku, note its pass closes 30 Sep 2026. Put the toll savings toward the road-trip ramen.
