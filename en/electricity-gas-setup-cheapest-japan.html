Set up electricity & gas in Japan — the cheapest way (and the one trap to avoid)

⚡ For foreign residents moving into a Japanese apartment who want to set up power and gas without overpaying by inertia.
⏱ 30-second answer
  • Since deregulation you can choose your electricity and gas retailer — not just the default regional utility (denki / gas). New retailers and set plans (electricity + gas bundled) plus points can cut the bill.
  • Setup is easy: usually no deposit, apply online ~1 week before move-in, and you mostly just need your address + start date (often no hanko needed).
  • The trap: some cheap plans are market-linked (price rises with wholesale rates) or have cancellation terms. If you want predictability, pick a fixed/standard set plan from a reputable retailer. See the full moving checklist →

Most people just call the regional utility, pay the default rate, and never look again. That inertia quietly costs money for years. Mio doesn't care about which brand is famous — here's the math.

You can actually choose — that's the whole point

Japan deregulated electricity in 2016 and gas in 2017. That means the old regional monopoly (TEPCO, Kansai Electric, Tokyo Gas, Osaka Gas, etc.) is now just one option. Dozens of new retailers resell the same grid power and pipeline gas — the electricity and gas quality is identical, only the plan and price change. Many offer an electricity + gas "set" plan that shaves a few percent off, plus loyalty points (many bill in yen-equivalent points you actually use).

How to set it up (simple, usually no deposit)

For a normal rental you generally do not pay a deposit for electricity or gas. Apply online (or by phone) roughly a week before move-in. You'll typically need your new address, move-in (start) date, and a payment method (card or bank). A hanko is usually not required for utility signup. One nuance: city gas often needs someone home for a short safety open-valve visit on the start day — book that slot in advance.

Already living somewhere on the default rate? You can switch retailers without moving and usually without any construction — the switch is handled on paper/online and your meter stays the same.

The trap most people miss: market-linked prices & cancellation terms

The cheapest-looking plans are sometimes market-linked (shijō rendō): the unit price tracks the wholesale electricity market, so it's cheap when the market is calm and spikes when it isn't. Great for gamblers, bad for budgeting. Also check for minimum contract periods or early-cancellation fees — some cheap plans lock you in, which matters if you'll leave Japan or move again soon.

If you value predictability: pick a standard (fixed unit-rate) set plan from a reputable retailer, skip market-linked plans, and confirm there's no cancellation fee before signing.

Mio's verdict

Don't default to the regional utility out of inertia. Pick an electricity + gas set plan from a reputable retailer, avoid market-linked plans if you want a predictable bill, and check for cancellation fees — especially if your stay is short. Setup is a 10-minute online form with no deposit; the payoff repeats every year.
