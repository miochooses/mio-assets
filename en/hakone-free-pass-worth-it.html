Hakone Free Pass (¥6,100): worth it if you do the loop — the math

♨️ For anyone doing Hakone from Tokyo, deciding between the Hakone Free Pass and paying per ride.
⏱ 30-second answer
  • The Hakone Free Pass (2-day from Shinjuku) is about ¥6,100 and covers the Odakyu round trip + the whole sightseeing loop: Tozan railway & bus, the ropeway, and the Lake Ashi pirate ship — plus discounts at museums/onsen.
  • Do the classic loop even once and the per-leg fares already add up past ¥6,100 — so for sightseers it's an easy win.
  • It's a waste only if you go straight to a ryokan and soak for two days without riding the loop. Tip: the 3-day pass is only ~¥400 more — worth it even for a 2-day trip. Also near Fuji →

Hakone is a loop, not a single stop: train up the mountain, switchback to the ropeway, cross the volcanic valley, sail Lake Ashi on the "pirate ship", then back. Paid separately, those legs add up fast. The Hakone Free Pass bundles them with the round-trip from Shinjuku — which is exactly why it's usually the right call for sightseers, and the wrong one for pure soakers.

When it wins — and when it doesn't

Buy it if you'll ride the loop (the vast majority of first-time visitors). Skip it if your plan is a car or shuttle straight to a ryokan for a hot-spring stay with no sightseeing — then you're paying for transport you won't use. And since the 3-day pass is only ~¥400 more than the 2-day, grab the 3-day even for a 2-day/1-night trip for the breathing room.

Mio's verdict

Doing the Hakone loop? Buy the Free Pass (~¥6,100) — one loop already beats paying per leg, and you get museum/onsen discounts too. Only skip it if you're heading straight to a ryokan to soak. Take the 3-day for ~¥400 more, and budget extra only if you want a reserved Romancecar seat.
