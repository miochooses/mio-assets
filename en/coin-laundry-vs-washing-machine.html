Coin laundry vs buying a washing machine in Japan — which is cheaper for your stay?

🧺 For anyone in Japan for a limited time (students, working-holiday, 1–3 year assignments) deciding how to do laundry.
⏱ 30-second answer
  • It's a break-even question, not a preference. Coin laundry ≈ ¥500–800 per load; a cheap washing machine ≈ ¥20,000–35,000 new.
  • Rough rule: if you'll stay more than ~8–12 months and do laundry weekly, buying usually wins. Shorter than that, the coin laundry is fine.
  • The number people forget: disposal. In Japan you pay a recycling fee to throw out an appliance — factor it in if you'll leave soon. See the full arrival checklist →

This is exactly the kind of small, everyday decision where "just feel it out" quietly costs you money. Mio doesn't care about vibes — here's the math.

The two costs, side by side

A coin laundry (coin randorī) load is typically a wash (¥300–500) plus a dryer (¥100 per ~10 min, so ¥200–400) — call it ¥500–800 per load, illustrative. A basic single-tub washing machine is often ¥20,000–35,000 new (cheaper secondhand at a recycle shop), and you dry on a rack for free.

So if you'll be in Japan less than about 8 months, the coin laundry likely wins. Longer than a year, buying wins — and by year two you've saved roughly ¥25,000–35,000 vs the coin laundry.

The trap most people miss: disposal

In Japan you can't just leave an appliance at the curb. Washing machines fall under recycling rules, and you typically pay a collection/recycling fee to dispose of one (varies by municipality and retailer; often a few thousand yen). If you buy and then leave Japan a few months later, that fee eats into the saving.

Smart middle path: buy secondhand from a recycle shop (¥8,000–15,000), and sell/give it away before you leave (Mercari, Sayonara Sales, or the shop's buyback) — that collapses both the purchase cost and the disposal cost.

Mio's verdict

Staying under ~8 months → coin laundry. Staying a year or more → buy (ideally secondhand and resell before leaving). The "right" answer isn't about convenience — it's about how long your meter is running.
