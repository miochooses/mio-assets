The Shinkansen ¥1,000 luggage trap — bags over 160cm need a (free) reservation, or you pay

🧳 For anyone boarding the Shinkansen with a big suitcase — before you get charged for something that's free if you book it.
⏱ 30-second answer
  • On the Tokaido, Sanyo & Kyushu Shinkansen (Tokyo–Osaka–Hiroshima–Fukuoka), a bag whose three sides added up (H+W+D) exceed 160cm needs a reserved "seat with oversized baggage area."
  • Booking that seat ahead of time is FREE — it's a normal reserved seat next to a luggage space. Bring an over-160cm bag without booking and it's a ¥1,000 handling fee.
  • Under 160cm total? Nothing to do — carry it on as normal. Over 250cm total isn't allowed.
  • The trap: it's the sum of all three sides, and many large check-in suitcases sit right at ~160cm. When in doubt, book the free oversized seat.

Since 2020, the busiest Shinkansen lines have controlled big suitcases with a simple system that quietly costs unprepared tourists ¥1,000. It's not a fare — it's a fee for bringing an oversized bag without the (free) reservation. Mio doesn't guess — here's the rule, read from the Japanese railways' own guidance, in plain English.

How to measure — it's the sum of three sides

Add height + width + depth of your bag. That single total decides everything:

Height + Width + Depth = your total≤160cm: carry on freely. 160–250cm: reserve the free oversized-baggage seat. Over 250cm: not permitted on board.

The catch is that it's the combined figure, not any one side. A typical large (L-size) check-in suitcase is often around 155–165cm total — right on the line — so plenty of holiday suitcases tip over 160cm without their owner realizing.

The reservation is free — the ¥1,000 is the penalty

If your bag is 160–250cm total, you book a "seat with an oversized baggage area" (特大荷物スペースつき座席). Key point: this is a normal reserved seat that happens to sit in front of a dedicated luggage space, and reserving it costs nothing extra. You only pay if you show up with an over-160cm bag and hadn't reserved — then staff charge a ¥1,000 handling fee and place the bag where space allows.

So the smart move is obvious: if there's any chance your suitcase is over 160cm, reserve the oversized seat when you book your ticket. Same seat comfort, zero extra cost, no risk of the fee.

Which trains this applies to

The rule currently covers the Tokaido, Sanyo, and Kyushu Shinkansen — i.e. the Tokyo ⇄ Nagoya ⇄ Kyoto ⇄ Osaka ⇄ Hiroshima ⇄ Hakata (Fukuoka) corridor and Kyushu, which is exactly where most tourists ride with luggage. Other Shinkansen networks (e.g. the Tohoku/Hokuriku side) don't use this specific reservation system, but always check for your route.

One more practical note: because the oversized space is tied to specific reserved seats, travelling with a big bag in a non-reserved car is awkward anyway — book reserved and you solve both problems at once.

How to book it

  • At a ticket machine or JR ticket window, ask for a reserved seat with the oversized baggage area.
  • Via the online reservation services (e.g. the Tokaido-Sanyo-Kyushu "smartEX/EX" booking), pick a seat flagged for oversized baggage.
  • Hold a rail pass? You still make a seat reservation and select the oversized-baggage seat — the seat reservation itself is included.

Mio's read

This is the rare "trap" with a free escape. Measure your bag's three sides added together; if it's over 160cm, reserve the oversized-baggage seat (free) when you buy your ticket, and you'll never see the ¥1,000 fee. Under 160cm, ignore all of this. It applies on the Tokaido/Sanyo/Kyushu lines most visitors use. Rules and fees can change — confirm the current details with JR when you book.
