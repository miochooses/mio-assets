Chubu Centrair (NGO) to Nagoya: do you really need the ¥1,430 μ-SKY?

✈️ For anyone landing at Nagoya's Chubu Centrair airport who wants the fastest-cheapest way into the city.
⏱ 30-second answer
  • The headline train is the μ-SKY limited express: ~28 minutes, ¥1,430 to Nagoya Station — but every car is a reserved seat, so ¥450 of that is a seat surcharge (the "μ-ticket").
  • The quiet money move: take a regular Meitetsu Limited Express and ride a non-reserved car for just the ¥980 base fare — same railway, only a little slower, and you save the ¥450.
  • Buses and taxis exist but cost more or aren't faster in traffic. For most visitors, ¥980 non-reserved is the value pick; pay ¥1,430 only if you want a guaranteed seat with luggage at a busy hour. Sort your yen first →

Chubu Centrair (NGO) is compact and the trains are easy — but the default everyone photographs, the sleek μ-SKY, isn't your only option, and it isn't the cheapest. The Meitetsu line runs both the all-reserved μ-SKY and ordinary limited expresses on the same tracks. Knowing the difference is worth ¥450 a person, each way.

When the μ-SKY is worth it

The ¥450 buys a guaranteed reserved seat and the slightly faster run. That's genuinely worth it if you're arriving at a busy time with big suitcases and don't want to stand, or you're tight on a connection. If you're relaxed, travelling light, or off-peak, the non-reserved car on a regular limited express gets you there for ¥980 — same destination, same railway.

Mio's verdict

Default to the ¥980 non-reserved limited express; upgrade to the ¥1,430 μ-SKY only when a guaranteed seat is worth ¥450 to you. Don't assume the flagship train is the only way into town — that assumption is exactly what the surcharge counts on. Check the next few Meitetsu departures, pick a regular limited express if the timing suits, and keep the ¥450. Then put it toward a bowl of Nagoya's miso katsu.
