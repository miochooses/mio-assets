Pocket WiFi vs home fibre vs tethering in Japan — which internet for your stay?

📶 For anyone in Japan for a limited time (students, working-holiday, 1–3 year assignments) deciding how to get online at home.
⏱ 30-second answer
  • It's driven by two numbers: how long you stay and how much data you burn. Not brand preference.
  • Short stay (<1 year) or light use → pocket WiFi or a large-data SIM + phone tethering. No installation, no removal, fewer contract traps.
  • Long stay + heavy use (WFH, streaming) → home fibre (hikari) is cheaper per GB, but expect weeks of lead time, a ~2-year contract, and cancellation/removal fees. Related: SIM without a Japanese card →

This is a recurring monthly bill, so a "just sign whatever" choice quietly costs you money every month for your whole stay. Mio doesn't care about vibes — here's the math.

The three options, side by side

Phone tethering uses the data on your SIM — great if your plan already has a large or "unlimited" allowance, but speeds and caps vary and it drains your battery. Pocket WiFi is a small rental router you carry; typically ¥3,000–4,000/month, no installation, works day one. Home fibre (hikari) is a wired line into your apartment: typically ¥4,000–6,000/month plus a setup/installation fee, with the lowest cost per GB but a real commitment.

The trap most people miss: auto-renew contracts

Many home-fibre and some pocket-WiFi plans lock you into a fixed term (commonly ~2 years) and then auto-renew. Cancel outside a short "free cancellation window" and you can owe an early-termination fee — and fibre often adds a separate line-removal / restoration fee when you move out. If you'll leave Japan before the term ends, those fees can wipe out the "cheaper per month" saving.

Mio-style defence: before signing, find three numbers — the contract length, the cancellation fee, and the renewal/free-cancellation month. If your stay is shorter than the term, prefer a no-contract or short-term option (pocket WiFi or tethering) even if the monthly figure looks higher.

Mio's verdict

Staying under a year, or light use → pocket WiFi or a large-data SIM + tethering. No installation, no removal, no contract trap. Staying 2+ years with heavy use → home fibre, but only after you've read the contract length and the exit fees. The "right" answer isn't about speed brochures — it's about how your stay length lines up with the contract term.
