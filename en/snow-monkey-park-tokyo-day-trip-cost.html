Snow monkeys from Tokyo: ¥800 to get in — but a ¥23,000, 12-hour day to get there

🐒 For anyone who pinned "snow monkeys" on the map assuming it's a quick side-trip from Tokyo.
⏱ 30-second answer
  • The park is cheap — the trip isn't. Jigokudani Monkey Park entry is just ¥800, but from Tokyo it's a ~12-hour round trip costing roughly ¥23,000 (Shinkansen + bus) for one adult.
  • From Nagano it's a bargain half-day. The Snow Monkey Pass (¥5,100) bundles the Nagaden train, the local bus, and park entry — so if you're already near Nagano, this is the clear winner, not piecing tickets together.
  • New for 2026: from August 2026 the park sells dated online tickets 3 months ahead, and on busy days people without one may not get in. Don't just turn up on a snowy weekend. More Tokyo day-trip math →

"Let's see the snow monkeys" sounds like an easy Tokyo add-on. It isn't. The famous bathing macaques live at Jigokudani Monkey Park in Nagano — and while the ¥800 entry is almost a rounding error, the time and transport are the real cost. Here's the honest math on doing it from Tokyo versus from Nagano, and which ticket actually saves you money.

Why ¥800 entry becomes a ¥23,000 day from Tokyo

The gate price is tiny. What you're really buying is distance: Tokyo → Nagano is about 90 minutes on the Hokuriku Shinkansen (roughly ¥8,000 each way reserved), then a bus of ~45 minutes to the trailhead, then a ~30-minute, 1.6 km walk uphill to the monkeys — each way. Round-trip that's a full ~12-hour day out of your trip.

The Express Bus Pass (¥6,800) already includes park admission and the direct round-trip bus from Nagano Station, so you don't pay the ¥800 gate again. Book the Shinkansen separately.

From Nagano, the ¥5,100 pass is the winner

If you're overnighting in or near Nagano (or on a Hokuriku route anyway), the trip stops being a marathon. The Snow Monkey Pass (¥5,100 adult) covers unlimited one-day Nagaden Railway travel + the local round-trip bus between Yudanaka and the park + one park admission. Buying each leg separately usually costs more and is fiddlier — the pass is both cheaper and simpler.

What about a guided day tour?

Guided round-trip tours from Tokyo or Nagano bundle the transport, the walk-in, and a guide into one booking. They cost more than DIY, but they remove the connections, the timing risk, and — increasingly important — they usually secure your dated entry for you. If you're not comfortable chaining Shinkansen → bus → snowy walk on a tight winter timetable, a tour can be worth the premium; if you are, DIY from Nagano is cheaper. Compare the tour's total price against ¥5,100 (from Nagano) or ~¥22,800 (from Tokyo) before you book.

The 2026 rule that can shut you out

From August 2026, Jigokudani sells dated online tickets up to 3 months ahead, and on busy days these take priority — visitors without one may be turned away. Winter weekends and holidays (peak snow-monkey season) are exactly when this bites. If your trip depends on seeing them, reserve the dated entry in advance, especially for December–February.

Mio's verdict

Don't treat this as a casual Tokyo half-day. From Tokyo it's a committed ~12-hour, ~¥22,800 day — only worth it if seeing the monkeys is genuinely a highlight for you. The smart play is to fold it into a Nagano/Hokuriku stop and buy the ¥5,100 Snow Monkey Pass, which bundles train, bus and entry and cuts the cost roughly four-fold. Either way, book the dated entry ahead for winter so you're not turned away at the gate.
