Kyoto's famous ¥700 bus day pass is gone — here's what to buy instead

🚌 For anyone whose Kyoto plan says "just get the ¥700 bus pass" — that advice is out of date.
⏱ 30-second answer
  • The ¥700 city-bus one-day pass was abolished — sales ended September 2023. Old guidebooks and blogs still push it, but you can't buy it anymore.
  • Now it's ¥230 a ride, or the ¥1,100 subway-and-bus one-day pass — which only pays off at roughly 5+ rides in a day.
  • For most sightseers, just tap an IC card (Suica/ICOCA) at ¥230 a ride. Two or three bus rides a day = ¥460–690, still under the ¥1,100 pass. IC card basics →

Kyoto scrapped its beloved ¥700 city-bus one-day pass to fight overcrowding — buses were so jammed with pass-holders that locals couldn't get on. The problem: half the Kyoto guides online still tell you to buy it. Here's what actually exists now, and the honest math on whether any pass beats just tapping a card.

The ¥1,100 pass covers Kyoto subway + City Bus + Kyoto Bus + Keihan Bus (some exceptions). It's a real deal if you ride a lot and use the subway — but it replaced a ¥700 pass, so the break-even jumped.

The ¥1,100 pass only wins at ~5 rides

At ¥230 a ride, the ¥1,100 pass breaks even at ¥1,100 ÷ ¥230 ≈ 4.8 rides — so you need about 5 bus/subway trips in one day for it to beat paying as you go. Most sightseeing days are 2–4 rides (you spend hours at temples, not on buses), which comes to ¥460–920 — under the pass. Unless you're temple-hopping hard or leaning on the subway, an IC card is cheaper and simpler.

Typical Kyoto day: 3 rides = ¥690 on an IC card vs ¥1,100 passBuy the pass only if you'll clock ~5+ rides or use the subway a lot. For a normal 2–4 ride day, just tap Suica/ICOCA and save the difference.

Mio's verdict

Ignore any guide that tells you to buy the ¥700 bus pass — it no longer exists. For a normal Kyoto day of 2–4 rides, tap an IC card at ¥230 a ride and skip passes entirely. Only buy the ¥1,100 subway-and-bus pass if you know you'll do 5+ rides or use the subway across the city. And because the buses are famously packed, plan some legs on the subway, on foot, or by bike — often faster than crawling through traffic anyway.
