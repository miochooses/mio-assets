Vending machine vs supermarket vs my tumbler — the daily drink habit in Japan, calculated

🥤 For anyone in Japan who buys a drink most days — students, commuters, working-holiday, anyone with a vending machine on the corner.
⏱ 30-second answer
  • Same bottle, three prices: vending machine ≈ ¥150–180, supermarket/drug store ≈ ¥80–110, home tea in a tumbler (mycha) ≈ ¥20–40.
  • One drink a day for a year: ~¥30,000–50,000 from vending machines vs a few thousand yen brewing your own. The gap is a used bicycle or a domestic flight.

This is exactly the kind of small, everyday decision where "just grab one" quietly costs you money. Mio doesn't care about the walk to the machine — here's the math.

The three prices, side by side

The same 500ml bottle of tea or water is priced completely differently by where you buy it. A vending machine (jidōhanbaiki) is the most expensive convenience: ¥150–180. The supermarket or drug store sells the identical bottle for ¥80–110 (often cheaper in a 6-pack). Brewing your own tea at home and carrying it in a tumbler (mycha, "my tea") costs roughly ¥20–40 per fill in tea leaves and water. Illustrative — prices vary by brand and area.

Switching from vending machine to supermarket saves roughly ¥23,000 a year for the same drink. Switching all the way to a tumbler saves closer to ¥45,000 a year — the price of a used bicycle or a domestic flight, spent ¥150 at a time.

The trap most people miss: it's too small to notice

¥150 doesn't register as a decision. It's below the threshold where your brain flags "should I?" — so it's never counted, never compared, never questioned. That's precisely why it adds up. A cost you don't notice is a cost you never optimize. The vending machine isn't expensive per press; it's expensive per year, and the year is invisible.

The honest nuance: convenience is real. On a 35°C August day, walking past the machine to hydrate is worth ¥150 — heatstroke costs more than money. If you're out and thirsty with no shop nearby, buy the drink. The point isn't "never use a vending machine." It's: know it's the ¥58,000/yr option, and make it the exception, not the default.

Mio's verdict

Default to a tumbler you brew at home; buy from the supermarket in packs; treat the vending machine as the emergency option. The "right" answer isn't about willpower — it's about which drink is quietly running your meter every single day.
