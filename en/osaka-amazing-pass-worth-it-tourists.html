Osaka Amazing Pass (¥3,300): worth it only if you do this — the break-even, calculated

🏯 For anyone with an Osaka day deciding whether to buy the Amazing Pass — or just tap an IC card.
⏱ 30-second answer
  • The pass is ¥3,300 (1-day) / ¥5,500 (2 consecutive days) and gives free entry to ~40 attractions plus unlimited Osaka Metro subway rides.
  • It only wins if you actually visit several paid attractions in the day and ride the subway a fair bit. Do 2–3 paid sights + subway and you're ahead; do fewer and you overpay.
  • Rough break-even: Osaka Castle (~¥600) + Umeda Sky Building (~¥1,500) + Tombori River Cruise (~¥1,200) + subway (~¥800) ≈ ¥4,100 > ¥3,300 → the pass wins that kind of day.
  • It loses on an eat-and-walk Dotonbori/shopping day (most of that is free), or if you only tick one attraction.

The Osaka Amazing Pass looks like a no-brainer — "~40 attractions free!" — but like every tourist pass, it's a bet: it only pays off if your day is heavy on paid sights and subway hops. Mio doesn't guess — here's the break-even so you buy it only when the math says yes.

What you actually get

  • Free entry to ~40 attractions — Osaka Castle, Umeda Sky Building observatory, Tombori River Cruise, Tsutenkaku, the Aqua Liner / Santa Maria cruises, and more, plus discounts at ~50 further spots.
  • Unlimited Osaka Metro subway (and city buses) for the 1 or 2 days — handy between Umeda, Namba, Shinsaibashi and Tennoji.
  • The 2-day pass must be two consecutive days; coverage of some private railways differs, so check the map for the spots you want.

The break-even, in one line

Add up the paid attractions you'll really visit + your subway rides. If that total > ¥3,300 (1-day), buy the pass.2–3 headline attractions + a few subway trips usually clears it. One attraction and lots of walking usually doesn't.

The trap is counting attractions you might see. Count only the paid ones you'll actually enter. Dotonbori's neon, street food, Shinsaibashi shopping and temple grounds are largely free — a day built around those doesn't need the pass.

Where to buy

The pass is sold at Osaka tourist information counters, some stations, and online travel-experience platforms (often as a scan-and-go QR). Compare the current price and what's included before you commit — attraction line-ups and fees change.

Mio's read

Treat the Osaka Amazing Pass as a math problem, not a souvenir. List the paid attractions you'll truly enter and your subway rides; if they beat ¥3,300, buy it — a castle + observatory + cruise day clears it easily. If your Osaka is mostly eating, walking and shopping, skip it and tap an IC card. Prices change — check the current pass price and entry fees before you buy.
