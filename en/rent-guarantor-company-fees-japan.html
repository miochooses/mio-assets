Japan's rent guarantor company fee (hoshō gaisha) — the move-in cost foreigners almost always pay, plus the yearly fee nobody mentions

🏠 For foreigners renting a private apartment in Japan who see a line called "guarantor company fee" (保証会社) on the quote and can't tell if it's optional.
⏱ 30-second answer
  • Most Japanese landlords now require a paid guarantor company (hoshō gaisha, 保証会社) that will cover your rent if you default. As a foreigner you'll almost always be routed to one — and the landlord usually picks the company, not you.
  • Two costs to plan for: an initial fee of ~50–100% of one month's rent at signing, then a recurring fee — commonly ~¥10,000 per year, or 10–30% of monthly rent yearly (some charge ~1–2% monthly instead). It doesn't disappear after move-in.
  • The trap: it's often required even if you already have a personal Japanese guarantor. Compare quotes and ask what the guarantor fee is before you commit. See the full arrival checklist →

Deposit, key money, agent fee — the big move-in numbers get all the attention. The guarantor company fee is the quiet one: small on the page, but it lands twice (once at signing, then again every year), and as a foreigner you rarely get to skip it. Mio doesn't guess; Mio reads the fine print and plans for both hits. Here's how it actually works.

What a guarantor company is (and why it's your problem)

Traditionally a Japanese landlord wanted a joint guarantor (rentai hoshōnin) — a financially responsible person in Japan who agrees to pay if you can't. Most foreigners don't have that, so the market shifted to guarantor companies (hoshō gaisha): a business that plays that role for a fee. If you miss rent, the company pays the landlord and then chases you. It protects the landlord — you pay for the privilege.

Two things surprise newcomers. First, the landlord or management company usually chooses the guarantor company, so you can't always shop for the cheapest. Second, it's frequently required even if you can produce a personal Japanese guarantor — some landlords insist on a company regardless, and for foreign applicants it's close to standard.

The two fees, in detail

1) Initial fee (at signing). Charged once when the guarantee contract starts. Industry sources put it at roughly 50–100% of one month's rent (some companies advertise a wider 30–100% band depending on your profile and the property). On an ¥80,000/month flat that's about ¥40,000–¥80,000 — separate from your deposit, key money and agent fee.

2) The recurring fee (the one nobody mentions). This is the part that keeps costing you. Common structures:

• Annual renewal fee — often around ¥10,000 per year (sometimes billed every one to two years, to line up with the standard lease-renewal cycle).
• Percentage of rent — some companies charge 10–30% of one month's rent per year instead of a flat ¥10,000.
• Monthly fee — a minority charge roughly 1–2% of rent every month across the lease term instead of a yearly bill.

Is it really mandatory?

Not by law — but in practice, yes, for most foreign renters. Guarantor companies are "not strictly mandatory," yet foreign applicants "often need one even when they have income or a company contract," because it's the landlord's condition, not a government rule. A handful of landlords still accept a joint guarantor instead of a company, and a small number of "no-guarantor" or foreigner-friendly listings exist — but expect the guarantor company to be the default on the quote.

How to spot it on a quote (mitsumori)

On your estimate (mitsumori, 見積書) look for lines like 保証会社 (guarantor company), 保証委託料 (guarantee entrustment fee), or "guarantor fee." Check whether the number shown is the initial fee only — the recurring/annual fee is often listed separately, or buried in the guarantee contract rather than the headline move-in total. If you only see one guarantor line, ask: "Is there also a yearly (or monthly) guarantor fee, and how much?"

How to reduce your move-in cost

Ask for the full fee schedule up front — initial and recurring — before you sign anything. Compare properties, not just companies: since the landlord picks the guarantor, the cheapest guarantor setup is effectively chosen by choosing the property. Look for foreigner-friendly / low-initial-cost listings, "no key money" or "zero-zero" (deposit/key-money-free) rooms, and UR/public housing (which typically don't require a guarantor company at all). Prefer a flat ~¥10,000/year renewal model over a 10–30%-of-rent or monthly-percentage model if you plan to stay several years — do the multi-year math.
