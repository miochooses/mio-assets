Furnishing your place in Japan: buy new, buy secondhand, or rent?

🛋 For anyone in Japan for a limited stay (students, working-holiday, 1–3 year assignments) setting up an empty room — fridge, washer, microwave, bed, desk.
⏱ 30-second answer
  • A full starter set is roughly ¥80,000–150,000 new, ¥30,000–60,000 secondhand, or a rental set from ~¥3,000–8,000/month. All illustrative — hedge everything.
  • For a 1–3 year stay, buy secondhand and resell before you leave is usually the cheapest. For a very short stay (a few months), rental or a furnished room often wins.
  • The number foreigners forget: disposal. Fridges, washers, TVs and ACs carry a legal recycling fee to throw away — so selling/giving them away before departure is what actually saves money. See the full arrival checklist →

Furnishing an empty Japanese apartment is one of the biggest one-off costs of arriving — and one where "just buy it new, it's easier" quietly overpays. Mio doesn't care about convenience. Here's the math for a stay with an end date.

The three ways to furnish a room

A basic starter set means five things: a compact fridge, a washing machine, a microwave, a bed (or futon), and a desk. You have three paths:

  • New — big electronics stores (Yamada, Bic, Nitori for furniture). Clean, warrantied, most expensive.
  • Secondhand — recycle shops (risaikuru shoppu, Hard-Off / Off-House), Mercari and Jimoty, and "Sayonara sales" where departing expats dump everything cheap.
  • Rental / subscription — appliance-and-furniture rental sets billed monthly; or just rent a furnished room from the start.

Why secondhand-then-resell usually wins for 1–3 years

The trick isn't just paying less up front — it's getting money back. If you buy a ¥40,000 secondhand set and resell most of it before you leave (Mercari, Jimoty, a Sayonara sale, or the recycle shop's buyback), your net cost can land well under the new-set price.

The disposal trap most foreigners miss

Here's the part almost nobody prices in. In Japan you cannot just leave big appliances at the curb. Four categories — fridges/freezers, washing machines/dryers, TVs, and air conditioners — are covered by the Home Appliance Recycling Law (kaden risaikuru), and you must pay a recycling fee plus a collection fee to dispose of each one (commonly a few thousand yen per item, varies by maker and retailer).

Other large furniture (beds, desks) counts as oversized waste (sodai gomi): you buy a disposal ticket from a convenience store and book a municipal pickup — again, a fee per item. So if you buy a fridge and washer new and then leave Japan, you can easily pay ¥5,000–10,000+ just to throw it all away, on top of what you paid to buy it.

The escape: plan your exit like a local. Sell or give the items away before you leave — Sayonara sales, Mercari, Jimoty, a recycle shop buyback, or a free curb-side / building-noticeboard giveaway (sotodashi-style, but follow your building and municipality rules — you can't just dump it). Recovering even a little resale value beats paying to discard.

Mio's verdict

Very short stay (a few months) → rental set or a furnished room. No buying, no reselling, no disposal fee — you just hand the key back. 1–3 years → buy secondhand and resell before you leave. 3+ years or you hate hassle → buying new can be justified, but budget for the disposal fee unless you'll resell. The "right" answer isn't about comfort — it's about how long your meter runs, and whether you plan your exit before you plan your entrance.
