Missed the last train in Japan — taxi, capsule hotel, manga café, or wait for first train?

🚃 For anyone in Japan who looked up at 00:15 and realized the platform gates just closed.
⏱ 30-second answer
  • It's a distance question, not a willpower question. Late-night taxi ¥3,000–8,000+ (with the after-midnight surcharge), capsule hotel ¥3,000–5,000, manga/net café night pack ¥1,500–2,500 (with a shower and free drinks), waiting for first train = ¥0 and miserable.
  • Rough rule: under a few km → taxi wins on time and cost. Far out → net café or capsule beats a ¥6,000 taxi.
  • The number people forget: the surcharge. Taxis run a ~20% late-night rate roughly 22:00–05:00, so the meter climbs faster than the daytime number in your head. See the full arrival checklist →

This is exactly the kind of tired, everyday decision where "just grab a taxi" quietly costs you ¥6,000. Mio doesn't care that it's late — here's the math.

The four options, side by side

A late-night taxi starts around ¥500 base and climbs ¥300–500 per km, plus a ~20% night surcharge — so a real ride home is often ¥3,000–8,000+ depending on distance (illustrative). A capsule hotel is ¥3,000–5,000 for a bed, shower, and lockers. A manga/net café night pack (roughly 8 hours) is ¥1,500–2,500 and usually includes a reclining seat or flat booth, free soft drinks, and often a shower. Waiting for the first train (~05:00) costs nothing but the night.

So the honest answer is "how far are you?" Close in, the taxi is genuinely the smart buy. Far out, a ¥2,000 net café plus a ¥400 first-train ticket beats a ¥7,000 taxi by a wide margin — and you get a shower out of it.

The trap most people miss: the meter after midnight

The daytime fare you remember isn't the fare you'll pay. From roughly 22:00 to 05:00, taxis apply a ~20% late-night surcharge, and traffic-free empty roads don't help because the meter also ticks on time when idling. A route that's ¥4,000 at noon can be ¥4,800+ at 1 a.m. — the exact moment your judgment is worst.

Smart middle path: if you're more than a few km out, walk to the nearest net café (they're everywhere near big stations), take the shower, sleep in the booth, and catch the first train. Total: ¥2,000 all-in vs a ¥6,000 taxi.

Mio's verdict

Under ~3 km → taxi. ~6 km or more → net café or capsule, then first train. And if you're doing this most weekends, the real fix isn't a cheaper option — it's leaving 30 minutes earlier. The cheapest last-train problem is the one you never have.
