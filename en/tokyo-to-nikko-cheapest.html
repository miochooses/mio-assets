Tokyo to Nikko: the ¥1,400 Tobu train, not the ¥5,680 JR route

⛩️ For anyone day-tripping from Tokyo to Nikko (Toshogu shrine, waterfalls) who wants the cheapest sensible way there.
⏱ 30-second answer
  • Go Tobu from Asakusa, not JR. The local train is about ¥1,400 one way; the comfy Limited Express (Spacia/Kegon/Revaty) is about ¥3,050 and ~1h50.
  • The JR route (Tohoku Shinkansen to Utsunomiya, then the JR Nikko line) runs about ¥5,680 — nearly double, and with a transfer. Only worth it if you hold a JR Pass.
  • The Nikko World Heritage Area pass (~¥3,000) is about the same as a plain Asakusa⇄Nikko return (~¥2,800) but adds the shrine-area buses — good value if you'll ride them. Have a JR Pass? →

Search "Tokyo to Nikko" and JR results show up first — but Nikko is Tobu's turf. The private Tobu line runs straight there from Asakusa for a fraction of the JR fare, and its Limited Express is a comfortable reserved ride. Going JR usually means paying nearly double for a Shinkansen leg plus a transfer.

Which Tobu ticket — and which pass?

Short on money, fine with a transfer and a bit longer? Take the Tobu local (~¥1,400). Want a direct, reserved, comfortable ride? The Limited Express (~¥3,050) is worth it. Visiting the shrines and using local buses? The Nikko World Heritage Area pass (~¥3,000) usually pays for itself versus buying the return plus individual bus fares. Heading further to Lake Chuzenji/Kegon Falls, look at the wider "All Area" pass instead.

Mio's verdict

Take Tobu from Asakusa — the local at ~¥1,400 or the Limited Express at ~¥3,050 — not the ~¥5,680 JR route. If you'll ride the shrine-area buses, the ~¥3,000 World Heritage pass is the tidy choice. Save JR only for JR Pass holders, and put the savings toward Nikko's yuba (tofu skin).
