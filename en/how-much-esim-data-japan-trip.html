How much eSIM data do you actually need for Japan? Most tourists overpay — the sizing, calculated

📱 For anyone buying a Japan eSIM and staring at 3GB / 10GB / "unlimited" plans, unsure which to pick.
⏱ 30-second answer
  • The stuff you use constantly in Japan — maps, messaging, train apps, web — sips data. The stuff that gulps it — video streaming, video calls, tethering a laptop — is optional.
  • Rough sizing: a typical sightseeing traveler burns ~0.3–1 GB/day. So a ~1-week trip is often fine on ~5–7 GB; heavy streamers/tetherers want more or a genuine unlimited.
  • "Unlimited" eSIMs usually mean high-speed up to a daily cap, then throttled — read the fair-use line. Many travelers overpay for unlimited they never use.
  • Wi-Fi is everywhere (hotels, konbini, stations), so real cellular use is lower than you'd guess. Size for maps + messaging, add a buffer, done. Related: eSIM vs pocket Wi-Fi →

eSIM sellers love "unlimited," because it sounds safe. But in Japan you're on Wi-Fi a lot (hotels, cafés, konbini, many stations and trains), and the apps you lean on — Google/Apple Maps, a translation app, a train router, messaging — are light. Mio doesn't guess — here's how to size your plan so you don't pay for gigabytes you'll never touch.

Size it by the day

A practical estimate for a sightseeing traveler who uses maps all day, messages, checks trains and browses — but streams video on Wi-Fi, not cellular — is roughly 0.3–1 GB per day. Multiply by your trip length and add a buffer:

Data needed ≈ (days) × (~0.5–1 GB) + buffer~1 week of normal sightseeing ≈ 5–7 GB for many travelers. Heavy streamers/tetherers or content creators: size up or go genuinely unlimited.

The "unlimited" catch

Most "unlimited" travel eSIMs are unlimited at reduced conditions: full speed up to a daily high-speed cap (say a couple of GB), then throttled for the rest of the day. That's fine for maps and chat, painful for video. If you buy "unlimited," read the fair-use / daily-cap line so you know what you're really getting — and don't pay an unlimited premium if a fixed 5–10 GB plan covers you.

Cut your cellular use (so a smaller plan works)

  • Download offline maps of your cities before you go — navigation then uses almost no data.
  • Stream and back up photos on Wi-Fi (hotel/café), not cellular.
  • Turn on your phone's data-saver and check which apps auto-play video.

Do these and even a modest plan lasts the whole trip.

Don't reflex-buy "unlimited." In Japan you're on Wi-Fi often and your heavy apps are optional, so a fixed plan sized to ~0.5–1 GB/day (roughly 5–7 GB for a week) covers most travelers for less.
