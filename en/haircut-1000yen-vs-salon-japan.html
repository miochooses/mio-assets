¥1,000 quick-cut vs a full salon in Japan — which one actually wins?

✂️ For anyone in Japan deciding where to get a haircut — especially if explaining what you want in Japanese feels like the hard part.
⏱ 30-second answer
  • For a standard men's cut, this isn't close. A quick-cut ≈ ¥1,200–1,500 for ~10 minutes; a full salon ≈ ¥4,000–7,000+ with wash and styling.
  • Cut monthly and the year adds up: quick-cut ≈ ¥15,000/yr vs salon ≈ ¥60,000+/yr — a gap of roughly ¥45,000.
  • The underrated feature for foreigners: the quick-cut needs almost no Japanese — a ticket machine and a photo. Salon is worth it for colour, perm, or complex styles. See the full arrival checklist →

This is exactly the kind of small, repeating decision where "just book somewhere nice" quietly costs you money every month. Mio doesn't care about ambience — here's the math, plus one factor the price tags don't show.

The two options, side by side

A quick-cut chain (QB House and similar) is a cut only: no shampoo, no blow-out styling, ~10 minutes, pay at a ticket machine on the way in. Typically ¥1,200–1,500, illustrative. A full salon (biyōin) runs ¥4,000–7,000+ and includes a consultation, wash, cut, and style — but that consultation is where the Japanese gets real.

So over a year the quick-cut saves roughly ¥45,000 for a standard cut. Over a two-year stay that's near ¥90,000 — enough that "which chair I sit in" is genuinely a money decision, not just a style one.

The angle the price tags miss: the language barrier

For a non-Japanese speaker, the quick-cut's "no conversation needed" is an underrated feature, not a downside. You buy a ticket, sit down, and point at a photo on your phone — the whole interaction can be near-silent. Many people quietly dread the salon consultation ("how much off the sides?", "what about the fringe?") in a language they're still learning, and put off haircuts because of it.

The salon earns its price when the outcome depends on nuance: colour, perm, or a complex layered style where a photo isn't enough and you need real back-and-forth. If that's your cut, pay for the consultation — a bad colour job is far more expensive to fix than the salon premium.

Mio's verdict

Standard men's cut → quick-cut, decisively. It wins on cost and on language friction at the same time, which is rare. Colour, perm, or complex styling → salon, where the consultation you'd otherwise avoid is exactly what you're paying for. The "right" answer isn't about how fancy the chair is — it's about how many yen, and how much Japanese, each cut actually costs you.
