Japan travel eSIM vs pocket WiFi vs roaming — which is cheapest for tourists?

🧳 For anyone visiting Japan and deciding how to get mobile data — a travel eSIM, a pocket WiFi rental, or roaming.
⏱ 30-second answer
  • Solo traveller with an eSIM-capable phone? Travel eSIM, every time. Roughly ¥1,000–3,000 for a week / ~10GB (illustrative), installs before you fly, nothing to return.
  • Travelling as a group or family? One pocket WiFi (~¥800–1,200/day) shares across 5+ phones, so the per-person cost drops below a separate eSIM each.
  • Roaming from your home carrier is usually the most expensive — only worth it for a very short trip. First step: check your phone actually supports eSIM. See all English guides →

This is exactly the kind of pre-trip decision where "I'll just sort it at the airport" quietly costs you money. Mio doesn't care about convenience marketing — here's the math.

The three options, side by side

1. Travel eSIM — if your phone supports eSIM, this is the cheapest and fastest. You buy a data plan online, install it before you fly, and it activates when you land. Roughly ¥1,000–3,000 for a week with ~10GB (illustrative). No physical device, no counter queue, nothing to hand back.

2. Pocket WiFi rental — a small hotspot device (~¥800–1,200/day) that shares one connection across 5+ phones. Best for groups/families or phones that don't support eSIM. The catch: you must pick it up and RETURN it at an airport counter, and carry and charge it all trip.

3. Home-carrier roaming — usually the most expensive per GB. Worth it only for a very short trip, or pure laziness. Convenient, rarely cheap.

Pocket WiFi per person ≈ (¥/day × trip days) ÷ people sharingIllustrative. A shared device splits cheaply across a group; a travel eSIM is billed per phone, so cost scales with headcount.

So a solo traveller with an eSIM phone pays far less with an eSIM — one device rental for a week costs more than one eSIM. But a family of 4 flips it: one pocket WiFi split four ways is cheaper per person than four separate eSIMs, and everyone stays on one connection.

The trap most people miss: eSIM support and the return counter

Two things quietly break the plan. First, not every phone supports eSIM — older or region-locked handsets can't install one, which forces you onto a physical SIM or pocket WiFi. Check your phone's settings before the trip. Second, a pocket WiFi is a rental: you have to return it, usually to an airport counter by a deadline, or pay a penalty — and you carry and recharge it every day.

Smart middle path: solo and eSIM-capable → travel eSIM. Group or non-eSIM phone → one pocket WiFi, shared. Decide before you fly so you're not paying roaming rates while you queue at arrivals.

Mio's verdict

Solo with an eSIM phone → travel eSIM, every time. Group or family → one pocket WiFi splits cheaper per person. Roaming → only for a very short trip.
